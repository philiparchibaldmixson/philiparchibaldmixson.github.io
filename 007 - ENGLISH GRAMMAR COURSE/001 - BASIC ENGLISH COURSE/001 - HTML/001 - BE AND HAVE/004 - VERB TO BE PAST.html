⏰ Verb "TO BE" - Past Tense ⏰

Learn Was and Were

🌟 Introduction to Past Tense

The past tense of the verb "to be" is used to describe situations, states, or conditions that existed in the past but are no longer true in the present. Unlike the present tense which has three forms (am, is, are), the past tense only has two forms: was and were.

Understanding the past tense of "to be" is essential for talking about past experiences, describing how things used to be, and narrating stories about events that have already happened. This verb form is one of the most commonly used in everyday English conversation.

📋 Past Tense Forms

The verb "to be" has only TWO forms in the past tense: was and were. Which form you use depends on the subject of the sentence.

Pronoun Past Form Example
I was I was a student last year.
You were You were at the party yesterday.
He / She / It was He was happy. She was tired. It was cold.
We were We were friends in school.
You (plural) were You were right about that.
They were They were teachers in 2020.

💡 Easy Memory Rule

WAS = I, He, She, It (singular subjects)
WERE = You, We, They (plural subjects and "you")

🎯 Uses of Past Tense "To Be"

The past tense of "to be" is used in various situations to talk about the past:

1. Past States & Conditions
To describe how someone or something was in the past

• I was happy yesterday.
• The weather was beautiful last week.
• They were excited about the trip.

2. Past Locations
To say where someone or something was in the past

• I was at home last night.
• She was in Paris in 2023.
• We were at the beach last summer.

3. Past Identity & Occupation
To describe what someone or something was before

• He was a teacher five years ago.
• I was a child when that happened.
• They were students in my class.

4. Age in the Past
To talk about someone's age at a specific time

• I was ten years old in 2015.
• She was twenty when she graduated.
• They were very young then.

🕐 Time Expressions with Past Tense

These time expressions are commonly used with the past tense of "to be" to indicate when something happened:

Yesterday
"I was busy yesterday."
Last (week/month/year)
"We were in Rome last month."
Ago
"She was here two hours ago."
In (year/month)
"They were students in 2020."
When I was...
"When I was young..."
At that time
"He was happy at that time."

💡 Important Note

Time expressions help clarify that you're talking about the past. They usually come at the end or beginning of the sentence.

❌ Negative Form (Past)

To make negative sentences in the past tense, add not after was/were. You can also use contractions.

Full Forms:

• I was not at school yesterday.
• You were not late for the meeting.
• He was not happy about the news.
• She was not in the office.
• We were not ready for the test.
• They were not at home last night.

Contracted Forms:

• I wasn't at school yesterday.
• You weren't late for the meeting.
• He wasn't happy about the news.
• She wasn't in the office.
• We weren't ready for the test.
• They weren't at home last night.

💬 Common Negative Sentences:

• It wasn't easy.
• The movie wasn't interesting.
• We weren't tired after the game.
• The weather wasn't good yesterday.

❓ Question Form (Past)

To form questions in the past tense, move was/were before the subject. This is the same pattern as the present tense.

Yes/No Questions:

• Was I late?
• Were you at the party?
• Was he your teacher?
• Was she angry?
• Were we on time?
• Were they happy?

Wh- Questions:

• Where were you yesterday?
• What was your favorite subject?
• Who was that person?
• How was your day?
• Why were you late?
• When was the meeting?

💡 Short Answers

Question: Were you at home last night?
Answer: Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.

Question: Was she tired?
Answer: Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't.

Question: Were they students?
Answer: Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.

🔄 Present vs. Past Comparison

Present → Past Changes:

Present: I am a student now.
Past: I was a student last year.

Present: They are happy today.
Past: They were happy yesterday.

Present: She is in London.
Past: She was in Paris last month.

Present: We are tired.
Past: We were tired after the trip.
