🔮 Verb "TO BE" - Future Tense 🔮

Learn Will Be and Going To Be

🌟 Introduction to Future Tense

The future tense of the verb "to be" is used to describe situations, states, or conditions that will exist in the future. Unlike the present and past tenses, the future tense has a simple structure: we use will be for all subjects (I, you, he, she, it, we, they).

The future tense is essential for making predictions, talking about plans, expressing intentions, and discussing events that haven't happened yet. It helps us communicate about what we expect or hope will occur in the days, months, or years ahead.

📋 Future Tense Forms with "WILL BE"

The future tense of "to be" uses WILL BE for ALL subjects. This makes it much simpler than present or past tense!

Pronoun Future Form Example
I will be I will be a doctor in the future.
You will be You will be happy with your decision.
He / She / It will be He will be 30 next year. She will be there soon.
We will be We will be ready in five minutes.
You (plural) will be You will be proud of yourselves.
They will be They will be famous someday.

💡 Easy Memory Rule

WILL BE is used for ALL subjects - no exceptions!
This is much simpler than present (am/is/are) or past (was/were).
Contraction: will + be = 'll be (I'll be, you'll be, he'll be, etc.)

🎯 Uses of Future Tense "To Be"

The future tense of "to be" is used in various situations to talk about the future:

1. Predictions & Expectations
To predict or expect something in the future

• It will be sunny tomorrow.
• The test will be difficult.
• They will be surprised by the news.

2. Future States & Conditions
To describe how someone or something will be

• I will be happy when I graduate.
• She will be tired after the journey.
• We will be ready soon.

3. Future Location
To say where someone or something will be

• I will be at the office tomorrow.
• She will be in New York next week.
• They will be home by 6 PM.

4. Future Age & Time
To talk about age or time in the future

• I will be 25 next month.
• It will be midnight soon.
• Tomorrow will be Friday.

🕐 Time Expressions with Future Tense

These time expressions are commonly used with the future tense to indicate when something will happen:

Tomorrow
"I will be busy tomorrow."
Next (week/month/year)
"She will be here next week."
In (time period)
"We will be ready in 10 minutes."
Soon
"It will be dinner time soon."
Later
"They will be home later."
In the future
"I will be a teacher in the future."
📝 Contractions in Context:

❌ Negative Form (Future)

To make negative sentences in the future tense, add not after will. The contraction is won't (will not).

Full Forms:

• I will not be late tomorrow.
• You will not be alone.
• He will not be at the meeting.
• She will not be available next week.
• We will not be ready on time.
• They will not be happy about this.

Contracted Forms (won't):

• I won't be late tomorrow.
• You won't be alone.
• He won't be at the meeting.
• She won't be available next week.
• We won't be ready on time.
• They won't be happy about this.

💡 Important Note

Won't is the most common way to express future negative in spoken English. It's short for "will not" but sounds more natural in conversation.

❓ Question Form (Future)

To form questions in the future tense, move will before the subject. The word "be" stays after the subject.

Yes/No Questions:

• Will I be late?
• Will you be at the party?
• Will he be the manager?
• Will she be ready on time?
• Will we be finished by 5 PM?
• Will they be there?

Wh- Questions:

• Where will you be tomorrow?
• What will the weather be like?
• Who will be at the meeting?
• How will it be possible?
• When will you be ready?
• Why will he be absent?

💡 Short Answers

Question: Will you be at home tonight?
Answer: Yes, I will. / No, I won't.

Question: Will she be there?
Answer: Yes, she will. / No, she won't.

Question: Will they be ready?
Answer: Yes, they will. / No, they won't.

🔄 Alternative: GOING TO BE

Another way to express the future is using "going to be". This form is used for plans and intentions that are already decided.

Structure: am/is/are + going to + be

• I am going to be a lawyer. (I've decided)
• You are going to be surprised!
• He is going to be late. (I can see he's running behind)
• She is going to be angry. (prediction based on evidence)
• We are going to be busy next week. (we have plans)
• They are going to be here soon. (they're on their way)

Will Be vs. Going To Be:

WILL BE: Used for spontaneous decisions, predictions without evidence, and promises
• "I think it will be sunny tomorrow." (prediction)
• "Don't worry, I will be there!" (promise)

GOING TO BE: Used for pre-planned intentions and predictions based on present evidence
• "I am going to be a doctor." (my plan/goal)
• "Look at those clouds! It's going to be rainy." (evidence: clouds)

💡 Quick Guide

Both forms are correct for future! In many cases, you can use either one. "Going to be" sounds more informal and is very common in spoken English.

📊 Complete Comparison: Present → Past → Future

All Three Tenses:

Present: I am a student now.
Past: I was a student last year.
Future: I will be a teacher next year.

Present: They are happy today.
Past: They were happy yesterday.
Future: They will be happy tomorrow.

Present: She is in London.
Past: She was in Paris last month.
Future: She will be in Tokyo next month.
