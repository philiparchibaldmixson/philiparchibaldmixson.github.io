🔮 THERE WILL BE - Future Tense 🔮

Express Future Existence and Predictions

🌟 Introduction to Future "There"

The future tense structure "there will be" is used to talk about things that will exist or happen in the future. Unlike the present tense which distinguishes between "there is" (singular) and "there are" (plural), the future form is much simpler: we use "there will be" for both singular and plural nouns.

This structure is essential for making predictions, talking about future plans, discussing upcoming events, and describing what will be available or present in the future. It helps us communicate expectations and forecasts about future situations with clarity and precision.

📋 Future Structure: There Will Be

The future form "there will be" is used for BOTH singular and plural nouns. This makes it very easy to use!

Form When to Use Example
There will be (singular) One thing or person (future) There will be a meeting tomorrow.
There will be (uncountable) Uncountable nouns (future) There will be rain this afternoon.
There will be (plural) Two or more things/people (future) There will be many guests at the party.
📝 Examples:

Singular:
• There will be a test next week.
• There will be a concert on Saturday.
• There will be an opportunity to ask questions.

Plural:
• There will be 50 people at the event.
• There will be many changes next year.
• There will be several options available.

Uncountable:
• There will be food at the party.
• There will be time to discuss it later.
• There will be traffic during rush hour.

💡 Easy Memory Rule

THERE WILL BE works for everything!
• Singular: There will be a book.
• Plural: There will be books.
• Uncountable: There will be water.

Contraction: There will be → There'll be (informal)

🎯 Uses of "There Will Be"

The future structure "there will be" has several important uses:

1. Future Predictions
To predict what will exist or happen

• There will be more opportunities in the future.
• There will be technological advances.
• There will be challenges ahead.
• There will be changes in the weather tomorrow.

2. Future Events
To announce or describe upcoming events

• There will be a party next Friday.
• There will be a meeting at 3 PM.
• There will be a festival in the summer.
• There will be elections next year.

3. Future Availability
To say what will be available or present

• There will be food and drinks at the event.
• There will be tickets available online.
• There will be space for everyone.
• There will be parking near the building.

4. Future Quantity & Numbers
To indicate how many will exist

• There will be 100 guests at the wedding.
• There will be three presentations today.
• There will be enough resources for everyone.
• There will be several new students next semester.

🕐 Time Expressions with Future

These time expressions are commonly used with "there will be" to indicate when something will happen:

Tomorrow
"There will be a test tomorrow."
Next (week/month/year)
"There will be changes next year."
In (time period)
"There will be results in two days."
Soon
"There will be news soon."
In the future
"There will be robots everywhere."
Later
"There will be time to talk later."
📅 Context Examples:

• There will be a holiday next Monday.
• There will be a full moon tonight.
• There will be new opportunities in 2026.
• There will be a surprise at the end.
• There will be important announcements this afternoon.

❌ Negative Form: There Will Not Be / There Won't Be

To make negative sentences in the future, add "not" after "will" or use the contraction "won't".

Full Form: There will not be

• There will not be classes tomorrow.
• There will not be any problems.
• There will not be enough time.
• There will not be tickets available.
• There will not be many people at the event.

Contraction: There won't be (more common)

• There won't be classes tomorrow.
• There won't be any problems.
• There won't be enough time.
• There won't be tickets available.
• There won't be many people at the event.

💬 Real-Life Negative Sentences:

• Don't worry, there won't be a test next week.
• There won't be traffic at this hour.
• There won't be any issues with the plan.
• Unfortunately, there won't be food at the meeting.
• There won't be time for questions today.

💡 Usage Note

"Won't" is the most natural way to speak in English. Use "will not" for more formal or emphatic situations.

❓ Question Form: Will There Be...?

To form questions, move "will" before "there." The word "be" stays after "there."

Yes/No Questions:

• Will there be a meeting tomorrow?
• Will there be food at the party?
• Will there be enough chairs?
• Will there be time to finish?
• Will there be many people there?
• Will there be parking available?

Wh- Questions (Information Questions):

• How many people will there be?
• What will there be to eat?
• When will there be an announcement?
• Where will there be a gathering?
• Why will there be a delay?
• Who will there be at the meeting?

💡 Short Answers

Question: Will there be a test next week?
Answer: Yes, there will. / No, there won't.

Question: Will there be enough food?
Answer: Yes, there will. / No, there won't.

Question: Will there be many guests?
Answer: Yes, there will. / No, there won't.

📝 Question Examples in Context:

• Will there be WiFi at the hotel?
• How many students will there be in the class?
• Will there be a discount for early registration?
• What time will there be a break?
• Will there be vegetarian options?

🔄 Alternative: There is/are Going To Be

Another way to express the future is using "there is/are going to be". This form is used for planned events or predictions based on present evidence.

Structure: There + is/are + going to be

Singular: There is going to be
• There is going to be a storm tonight. (I can see dark clouds)
• There is going to be a problem. (I can see signs of trouble)
• There is going to be a party on Friday. (it's planned)

Plural: There are going to be
• There are going to be changes in the company.
• There are going to be many guests at the wedding.
• There are going to be three speakers today.

Will Be vs. Going To Be:

THERE WILL BE: General predictions, spontaneous decisions
• "I think there will be 50 people." (general guess)
• "There will be opportunities in the future." (prediction)

THERE IS/ARE GOING TO BE: Plans or predictions with evidence
• "There is going to be a meeting." (it's scheduled)
• "Look at those clouds! There is going to be rain." (evidence)

💡 Quick Guide

Both forms are correct! "There will be" is simpler and works in most situations. "There is/are going to be" shows more certainty or planning.

📊 Complete Overview: All Tenses Together

Present → Past → Future:

Present: There is a party today.
Past: There was a party yesterday.
Future: There will be a party tomorrow.

Present: There are many students here.
Past: There were many students here.
Future: There will be many students here.

Present Negative: There is no time now.
Past Negative: There was no time yesterday.
Future Negative: There won't be time tomorrow.

Present Question: Is there a problem?
Past Question: Was there a problem?
Future Question: Will there be a problem?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ There will are many people.
✅ There will be many people.

❌ Will there are a test?
✅ Will there be a test?

❌ There going to be a party.
✅ There is going to be a party.

❌ There will be have a meeting.
✅ There will be a meeting.

❌ There won't any problems.
✅ There won't be any problems.

❌ There will be have time later.
✅ There will be time later.
