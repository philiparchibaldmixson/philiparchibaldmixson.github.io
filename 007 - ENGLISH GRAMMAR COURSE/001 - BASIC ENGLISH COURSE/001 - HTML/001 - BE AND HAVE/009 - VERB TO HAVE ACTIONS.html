🎬 VERB "TO HAVE" FOR ACTIONS 🎬

Using "Have" to Describe Activities and Experiences

🌟 What Does "To Have" Mean in Actions?

The verb "to have" is often used to describe actions, events, and experiences. In this case, it doesn’t mean possession — it means doing or experiencing something.

We use "have" with certain nouns to talk about common activities like meals, conversations, meetings, and more. These are called collocations — words that naturally go together.

📋 Common Action Collocations with "Have"

Expression Meaning Example
Have breakfast/lunch/dinner Eat a meal We had lunch at 1 PM.
Have a meeting Attend or organize a meeting They will have a meeting tomorrow.
Have fun Enjoy yourself Did you have fun at the party?
Have a conversation Talk with someone Let’s have a conversation about your goals.
Have a break Take a rest I need to have a break after this.
Have a shower Wash yourself He had a shower before work.

💡 Memory Tip

Think of "have" as a helper verb that brings an activity to life:
• Have a meal → eat
• Have a chat → talk
• Have fun → enjoy

🎯 Real-Life Uses of "Have" for Actions

1. Social Activities
To describe interactions and events

• We had a great time at the beach.
• She had a long conversation with her friend.
• They had a party last weekend.

2. Daily Routines
To describe everyday actions

• I have breakfast at 7 AM.
• He had a shower before school.
• We have dinner together every night.

3. Work and Study
To describe professional or academic actions

• She had a meeting with her boss.
• We will have a test next week.
• They had a training session yesterday.

🕐 Time Expressions with Action "Have"

Every day
"I have coffee every morning."
Yesterday
"We had a meeting yesterday."
Tomorrow
"She will have an interview tomorrow."
Later
"Let’s have a chat later."
Now
"I’m having lunch right now."

❌ Negative Form

Examples

• I didn’t have time to eat.
• She doesn’t have a meeting today.
• We won’t have a break until 3 PM.

❓ Question Form

Examples

• Did you have fun?
• Do they have a meeting now?
• Will we have lunch together?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ She have a shower.
✅ She has a shower.

❌ Did you has fun?
✅ Did you have fun?

❌ We doesn’t have a meeting.
