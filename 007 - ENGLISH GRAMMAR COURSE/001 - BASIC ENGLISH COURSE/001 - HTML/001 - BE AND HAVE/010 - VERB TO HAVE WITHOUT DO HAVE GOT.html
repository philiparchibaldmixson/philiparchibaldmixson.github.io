🧩 VERB "HAVE GOT" 🧩

Expressing Possession Without Using "Do"

🌟 What Is "Have Got"?

"Have got" is a common way to express possession in English, especially in British English. It means the same as "have" but is used without the auxiliary verb "do/does" in questions and negatives.

It’s mostly used in the present tense and is very natural in everyday conversation. Americans often use "have" instead, but "have got" is widely understood and accepted.

📋 Structure of "Have Got"

Form Subject Example
Affirmative I / You / We / They I have got a bike.
Affirmative He / She / It She has got a cat.
Negative All subjects He hasn’t got a car.
Question All subjects Have you got a pen?

💡 Quick Tip

Have got = Have
Use it for possessions, relationships, and characteristics:
• I’ve got two sisters.
• She’s got blue eyes.
• We’ve got a problem.

🎯 Uses of "Have Got"

1. Possession
To show ownership

• I’ve got a new phone.
• They’ve got a big house.
• He’s got a laptop.

2. Relationships
To describe family or connections

• I’ve got two brothers.
• She’s got a lot of friends.
• We’ve got a good teacher.

3. Characteristics
To describe physical or personality traits

• He’s got green eyes.
• They’ve got curly hair.
• I’ve got a strong accent.

🕐 Time Use: Present Only

"Have got" is used only in the present tense. For past or future, use "had" or "will have".

Present
"I’ve got a cold."
Past
"I had a cold yesterday."
Future
"I’ll have a cold tomorrow." (not common)

❌ Negative Form

Examples

• I haven’t got any money.
• She hasn’t got a car.
• We haven’t got time.

❓ Question Form

Examples

• Have you got a pencil?
• Has he got a dog?
• Have they got a problem?

💡 Short Answers

Question: Have you got a phone?
Answer: Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t.

🔄 "Have Got" vs "Have"

Comparison

Have got: I’ve got a bike. (British, informal)
Have: I have a bike. (American, formal)

Both are correct in the present tense. "Have got" is more common in spoken British English.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ Do you have got a pen?
✅ Have you got a pen?

❌ She have got a dog.
✅ She has got a dog.

❌ I hasn’t got time.
✅ I haven’t got time.

Use "Have Got" for Present Possession!

It’s simple, natural, and perfect for everyday English!
