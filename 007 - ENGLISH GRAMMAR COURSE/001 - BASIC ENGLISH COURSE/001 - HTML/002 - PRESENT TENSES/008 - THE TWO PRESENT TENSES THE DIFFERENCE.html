📚 PRESENT TENSES – DIFFERENCES 📚

Understanding Present Simple, Progressive, and Perfect

🌟 Overview of Present Tenses

English has three main present tenses. Each one expresses a different kind of action or situation:

  • Present Simple: Regular actions or general truths
  • Present Progressive: Actions happening now or temporarily
  • Present Perfect: Actions that happened at an unspecified time or started in the past and continue

📋 Structure Comparison

Tense Structure Example
Present Simple Subject + base verb / verb + s She works every day.
Present Progressive Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing She is working now.
Present Perfect Subject + have/has + past participle She has worked here since 2020.

🎯 When to Use Each Tense

1. Present Simple
For routines, habits, and facts

• I wake up at 6 AM.
• She teaches English.
• Water boils at 100°C.
• They live in Bahia.

2. Present Progressive
For actions happening now or temporarily

• I am talking to you.
• She is cooking dinner.
• They are staying in Itamaraju this week.
• We are learning English.

3. Present Perfect
For actions with present relevance or unfinished time

• I have finished my homework.
• She has visited Salvador twice.
• We have lived here for five years.
• They have never eaten sushi.

🕐 Time Expressions

Present Simple
Always, usually, often, every day
Present Progressive
Now, at the moment, today, this week
Present Perfect
Already, yet, just, since, for, ever, never

📌 Quick Comparison

Same Verb – Different Tenses

Present Simple: I work every day.
Present Progressive: I am working right now.
Present Perfect: I have worked here for two years.

🔄 Common Mistakes

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ I working now.
✅ I am working now.

❌ She has eat lunch.
✅ She has eaten lunch.

❌ They goes to school.
✅ They go to school.

❌ We are live in Bahia.
✅ We live in Bahia.

Master the Present Tenses!

Use Simple for routines, Progressive for now, and Perfect for past actions with present impact!
