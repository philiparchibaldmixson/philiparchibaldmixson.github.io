🔮 FUTURE WITH "GOING TO" 🔮

Talking About Plans and Predictions

🌟 What Is "Going To" Future?

We use "going to" to talk about the future when we have a plan or when something is likely to happen based on evidence. It’s a very common and natural way to express future intentions and predictions.

The structure is: Subject + am/is/are + going to + base verb

📋 Structure of "Going To"

Form Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + am/is/are + going to + verb I am going to travel next week.
Negative Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + verb She is not going to study tonight.
Question Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + verb? Are they going to play soccer?

💡 Quick Tip

I → am
He/She/It → is
You/We/They → are
Then add going to + base verb

🎯 Uses of "Going To"

1. Future Plans
To talk about things we already decided to do

• I’m going to visit my grandmother.
• We’re going to study English tonight.
• She’s going to start a new job.
• They’re going to travel to Bahia.

2. Predictions with Evidence
To say what we think will happen based on what we see

• Look at those clouds! It’s going to rain.
• He’s going to fall — he’s not careful.
• The baby is going to cry.
• You’re going to love this movie.

📝 More Examples

✅ Affirmative

• I’m going to call you later.
• She’s going to cook dinner.
• We’re going to play volleyball.
• They’re going to visit Itacaré.

🚫 Negative

• I’m not going to sleep early.
• He’s not going to come today.
• We’re not going to watch TV.
• They’re not going to stay long.

❓ Questions

• Are you going to study tonight?
• Is she going to travel tomorrow?
• Are they going to eat now?
• What are you going to do?

📌 Time Expressions with "Going To"

Tomorrow
"I’m going to visit my aunt tomorrow."
Next week/month/year
"She’s going to start college next year."
Soon
"We’re going to leave soon."
In a few minutes
"They’re going to arrive in a few minutes."
Later
"I’m going to call you later."

🔄 "Going To" vs "Will"

Comparison

Going to: Plans or predictions with evidence
• "I’m going to study tonight." (planned)
• "Look! It’s going to rain." (evidence)

Will: Spontaneous decisions or general predictions
• "I’ll help you." (decision now)
• "It will be sunny tomorrow." (forecast)

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ I going to eat.
✅ I’m going to eat.

❌ She going to travel.
✅ She’s going to travel.

❌ Are going to play?
✅ Are you going to play?

❌ We not going to study.
✅ We’re not going to study.
