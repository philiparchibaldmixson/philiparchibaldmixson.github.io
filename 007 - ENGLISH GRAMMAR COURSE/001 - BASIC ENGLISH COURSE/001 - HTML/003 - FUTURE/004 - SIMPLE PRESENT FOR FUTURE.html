🕘 SIMPLE PRESENT FOR FUTURE 🕘

Talking About Scheduled and Fixed Events

🌟 What Is Simple Present for Future?

We usually use the Simple Present to talk about routines and facts. But it can also be used to talk about the future — especially when referring to scheduled events, timetables, or fixed plans.

This is common with transportation, school schedules, programs, and official events.

📋 Structure of Simple Present for Future

Subject Verb Example
I / You / We / They Base verb We leave at 7 AM tomorrow.
He / She / It Verb + s/es The train arrives at 6 PM.

💡 Quick Tip

Use the Simple Present for scheduled events that are part of a timetable or calendar.

🎯 When to Use Simple Present for Future

1. Transportation Schedules
To talk about buses, trains, flights, etc.

• The bus leaves at 8:30.
• My flight departs at noon.
• The train arrives at 5 PM.
• The ferry crosses every morning.

2. School and Work Timetables
To talk about scheduled classes or shifts

• School starts at 7:30 tomorrow.
• The meeting begins at 10 AM.
• Our class ends at 4 PM.
• The exam takes place on Monday.

3. Official Programs and Events
To talk about public or fixed events

• The movie starts at 9 PM.
• The concert begins at 8.
• The parade happens on Sunday.
• The store opens at 10 AM.

📝 More Examples

✅ Affirmative

• The train leaves at 6.
• My class starts at 7.
• The show begins at 9.
• We arrive in Salvador on Friday.

🚫 Negative

• The bus doesn’t stop here tomorrow.
• My class doesn’t start at 8.
• The store doesn’t open on Sundays.
• The flight doesn’t depart today.

❓ Questions

• What time does the movie start?
• Does the train leave at 5?
• When does school begin?
• Do we meet at 10 tomorrow?

📌 Time Expressions

Tomorrow
"The bus leaves tomorrow morning."
Next week
"The course starts next week."
On Monday
"The exam takes place on Monday."
At 7 PM
"The movie begins at 7 PM."
Every morning
"The ferry crosses every morning."

🔄 Simple Present vs Other Future Forms

Comparison

Simple Present: For fixed schedules
• "The train leaves at 6."

Going to: For plans
• "I’m going to visit my aunt."

Will: For decisions or predictions
• "I’ll call you later."

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ The train is leave at 6.
✅ The train leaves at 6.

❌ My class will starts at 7.
✅ My class starts at 7.

❌ Does starts the movie at 9?
✅ Does the movie start at 9?

❌ We going to arrive tomorrow.
✅ We arrive tomorrow.
