📘 SIMPLE PAST – USE 📘

Talking About Actions That Happened in the Past

🌟 What Is the Simple Past Used For?

The Simple Past is used to describe actions or situations that started and finished in the past. It’s perfect for telling stories, talking about past routines, and describing completed events.

We use it with regular verbs (add -ed) and irregular verbs (special forms).

🎯 Main Uses of the Simple Past

1. Completed Actions
To describe something that happened and ended

• I watched a movie last night.
• She cleaned her room.
• They traveled to Bahia.
• We studied English yesterday.

2. Past Routines
To describe habits or repeated actions in the past

• He walked to school every day.
• We played soccer after class.
• She worked at a bakery.
• I visited my uncle every summer.

3. Stories and Narratives
To tell what happened in a sequence

• The dog ran away.
• I opened the door and saw a surprise.
• She smiled and said hello.
• They arrived, ate dinner, and left.

4. Facts About the Past
To state something that was true before

• Brazil hosted the World Cup in 2014.
• Einstein developed the theory of relativity.
• My grandfather lived in Itamaraju.
• The school opened in 1990.

📝 More Examples

✅ Affirmative

• I studied last night.
• He visited his cousin.
• We watched a movie.
• They arrived early.

🚫 Negative

• I didn’t study last night.
• He didn’t visit his cousin.
• We didn’t watch a movie.
• They didn’t arrive early.

❓ Questions

• Did you study last night?
• Did he visit his cousin?
• Did we watch a movie?
• Did they arrive early?

📌 Time Expressions with Simple Past

Yesterday
"I visited my grandma yesterday."
Last night
"We watched a movie last night."
Two days ago
"She called me two days ago."
In 2020
"They moved to Bahia in 2020."
When I was a child
"I played outside when I was a child."

🔄 Simple Past vs Present Perfect

Comparison

Simple Past: Action is finished
• "I visited Salvador last year."

Present Perfect: Action has relevance now
• "I have visited Salvador before."

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ He go to school.
✅ He went to school.

❌ I didn’t went there.
✅ I didn’t go there.

❌ Did she went home?
✅ Did she go home?

❌ We was happy.
✅ We were happy.

Use the Simple Past to Talk About Finished Actions!

It’s perfect for stories, routines, and facts from the past!
