🎯 FINISHED ACTIONS: PRESENT PERFECT OR SIMPLE PAST? 🎯

Understanding Which Tense to Use

🌟 What’s the Difference?

Both Present Perfect and Simple Past can describe finished actions, but they are used in different contexts:

  • Simple Past: Action happened at a specific time in the past.
  • Present Perfect: Action happened at an unspecified time or has present relevance.

📋 Structure Comparison

Tense Structure Example
Simple Past Subject + past verb She visited Salvador last year.
Present Perfect Subject + have/has + past participle She has visited Salvador.

💡 Quick Tip

Use Simple Past when the time is clear. Use Present Perfect when the time is not mentioned or still relevant.

🎯 When to Use Each Tense

1. Specific Time → Simple Past
Use when the action happened at a known time

• I saw that movie last night.
• She traveled to Bahia in 2022.
• We met yesterday.
• They finished the project two days ago.

2. Unspecified Time → Present Perfect
Use when the time is not mentioned or not important

• I’ve seen that movie.
• She has traveled to Bahia.
• We’ve met before.
• They’ve finished the project.

3. Present Relevance → Present Perfect
Use when the result affects the present

• I’ve lost my keys. (I don’t have them now)
• He has broken his phone. (It doesn’t work)
• We’ve cleaned the room. (It’s clean now)
• She has finished her homework. (It’s done)

📝 More Examples

✅ Simple Past

• I visited Salvador last year.
• He called me yesterday.
• We watched that movie on Friday.
• They arrived at 8 PM.

✅ Present Perfect

• I’ve visited Salvador.
• He has called me.
• We’ve watched that movie.
• They’ve arrived.

📌 Time Expressions

Simple Past
Yesterday, last week, in 2020, two days ago, on Monday
Present Perfect
Ever, never, already, yet, just, recently, so far, before

🔄 Mixed Sentences

Combining Both Tenses

• I’ve read that book, but I read it last year.
• She has visited Bahia, and she went there in 2022.
• We’ve finished the homework, and we did it this morning.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ I have saw that movie.
✅ I have seen that movie.

❌ She has went to school.
✅ She has gone to school.

❌ We didn’t watched it.
✅ We didn’t watch it.

❌ Have you went there?
✅ Have you gone there?

Use Simple Past for Specific Time and Present Perfect for Unspecified Time or Present Relevance!

Practice with different examples to master the difference!
