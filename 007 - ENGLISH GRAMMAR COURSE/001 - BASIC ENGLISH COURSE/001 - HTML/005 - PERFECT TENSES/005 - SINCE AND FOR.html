⏱ SINCE & FOR – PRESENT PERFECT ⏱

Talking About Duration of Actions

🌟 What’s the Difference?

We use since and for with the Present Perfect to talk about how long something has been happening.

  • Since: Refers to the starting point of the action.
  • For: Refers to the duration of the action.

📋 Structure with Present Perfect

Expression Structure Example
Since have/has + past participle + since + point in time I’ve lived here since 2010.
For have/has + past participle + for + duration She has worked here for five years.

💡 Quick Tip

Use since with a specific date or moment.
Use for with a period of time.

🎯 Examples in Context

1. Using "Since"
To show when the action started

• I’ve lived in Itamaraju since 2015.
• She has worked here since January.
• We’ve been friends since childhood.
• They’ve studied English since last year.

2. Using "For"
To show how long the action has lasted

• I’ve lived in Itamaraju for 10 years.
• She has worked here for six months.
• We’ve been friends for a long time.
• They’ve studied English for two semesters.

📝 More Examples

✅ With "Since"

• I’ve known her since 2018.
• He has lived in Bahia since last summer.
• We’ve had this car since March.
• She has studied here since she was a child.

✅ With "For"

• I’ve known her for five years.
• He has lived in Bahia for six months.
• We’ve had this car for a long time.
• She has studied here for many years.

📌 Common Time Expressions

Since
2010, last week, Monday, childhood, I was a kid
For
two hours, three days, ten years, a long time, several months

🔄 Mixed Sentences

Combining Both

• I’ve worked here since 2020.
• I’ve worked here for three years.
• She has lived in Salvador since last summer.
• She has lived in Salvador for six months.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ I’ve lived here for 2010.
✅ I’ve lived here since 2010.

❌ She has worked here since five years.
✅ She has worked here for five years.

❌ We’ve been friends for last year.
✅ We’ve been friends since last year.

❌ They’ve studied English since two semesters.
✅ They’ve studied English for two semesters.

Use "Since" for Starting Points and "For" for Durations!
