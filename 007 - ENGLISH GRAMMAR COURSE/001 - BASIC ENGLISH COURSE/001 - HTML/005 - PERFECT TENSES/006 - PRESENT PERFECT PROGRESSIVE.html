⏳ PRESENT PERFECT PROGRESSIVE ⏳

Describing Actions That Started in the Past and Continue Now

🌟 What Is the Present Perfect Progressive?

The Present Perfect Progressive (also called Present Perfect Continuous) is used to describe actions that started in the past and are still happening or have recently stopped but have present relevance.

It is formed with have/has + been + verb-ing.

📋 Structure of Present Perfect Progressive

Form Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + have/has + been + verb-ing She has been studying all day.
Negative Subject + have/has + not + been + verb-ing They haven’t been working lately.
Question Have/Has + subject + been + verb-ing? Have you been sleeping well?

💡 Quick Tip

I / You / We / They → have been
He / She / It → has been
Then add verb + ing

🎯 When to Use the Present Perfect Progressive

1. Actions Continuing Until Now
To describe actions that started in the past and are still happening

• I’ve been studying English for two hours.
• She has been working here since 2020.
• We’ve been waiting for the bus.
• They’ve been living in Bahia for years.

2. Actions Recently Stopped
To describe actions that just finished but have present results

• I’ve been running — that’s why I’m tired.
• He has been cooking — the kitchen smells great.
• We’ve been cleaning the house.
• She has been studying — the books are still open.

📝 More Examples

✅ Affirmative

• I’ve been reading a book.
• He has been watching TV.
• We’ve been talking for hours.
• They’ve been learning English.

🚫 Negative

• I haven’t been sleeping well.
• She hasn’t been working lately.
• We haven’t been practicing.
• They haven’t been studying much.

❓ Questions

• Have you been studying?
• Has he been working today?
• What have they been doing?
• How long has she been waiting?

📌 Time Expressions

Since
"I’ve been working since 8 AM."
For
"She has been studying for three hours."
Lately / Recently
"We’ve been traveling a lot lately."
All day / All week
"They’ve been working all day."

🔄 Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Progressive

Comparison

• I’ve read five books. (Present Perfect – result)
• I’ve been reading all afternoon. (Present Perfect Progressive – activity)

• She has cleaned the house. (Present Perfect – finished)
• She has been cleaning the house. (Present Perfect Progressive – ongoing or recent)

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ He have been working.
✅ He has been working.

❌ I has been studying.
✅ I have been studying.

❌ We been learning English.
✅ We have been learning English.

❌ Has you been sleeping?
✅ Have you been sleeping?

Use Present Perfect Progressive to Talk About Ongoing or Recently Finished Actions!
