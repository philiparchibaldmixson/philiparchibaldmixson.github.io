🗣️ MODAL VERBS – INTRODUCTION 🗣️

Expressing Ability, Permission, Advice, Possibility and More

🌟 What Are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are special verbs that help us express ideas like ability, permission, necessity, possibility, and advice. They are followed by the base form of the main verb and do not change form.

Common modal verbs include: can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would.

📋 Structure of Modal Verbs

Modal Structure Example
Can Subject + can + base verb I can swim.
Should Subject + should + base verb You should study more.
Must Subject + must + base verb We must wear a helmet.

💡 Quick Tip

Modal verbs are always followed by the base form of the verb. No “to”, no “-ing”, no “s”.

🎯 Common Uses of Modal Verbs

1. Ability
Use "can" or "could"

• I can speak English.
• She could play the piano when she was 5.

2. Permission
Use "can", "may", or "could"

• Can I go to the bathroom?
• May I borrow your pen?
• Could we leave early today?

3. Advice
Use "should" or "ought to"

• You should eat more vegetables.
• He ought to study harder.

4. Obligation
Use "must" or "have to"

• We must wear uniforms.
• You have to finish your homework.

5. Possibility
Use "might", "may", or "could"

• It might rain today.
• She may come later.
• We could win the game.

📝 More Examples

✅ Affirmative

• I can help you.
• She should study.
• We must leave now.
• They might visit us.

🚫 Negative

• I can’t swim.
• He shouldn’t eat so much.
• We mustn’t be late.
• They might not come.

❓ Questions

• Can you help me?
• Should I call her?
• Must we go now?
• Might they join us?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ He can to swim.
✅ He can swim.

❌ She should to study.
✅ She should study.

❌ We must to go.
✅ We must go.

❌ Can you to help me?
✅ Can you help me?
