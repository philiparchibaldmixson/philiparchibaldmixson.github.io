📚 MODAL VERB: HAVE TO 📚

Expressing Obligation and Necessity

🌟 What Does "Have to" Mean?

HAVE TO is used to express obligation or necessity, similar to must. It shows that something is required or expected, often because of rules or external factors.

Unlike “must”, “have to” changes with the subject and tense.

📋 Structure with "Have to"

Form Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + have/has to + base verb She has to study tonight.
Negative Subject + don’t/doesn’t have to + base verb We don’t have to work tomorrow.
Question Do/Does + subject + have to + base verb? Do you have to leave now?

💡 Quick Tip

Use have to for general obligations. Use must for strong personal or official rules.

🎯 Common Uses of "Have to"

1. Daily Responsibilities
To talk about regular duties

• I have to wake up early.
• She has to cook dinner.
• We have to clean the house.
• They have to study for the test.

2. Rules and Laws
To express legal or official obligations

• You have to wear a seatbelt.
• He has to pay taxes.
• We have to follow the rules.
• Students have to bring their ID.

3. Scheduled Tasks
To talk about planned or necessary actions

• I have to go to the dentist.
• She has to finish her homework.
• We have to attend the meeting.
• They have to catch the bus.

📝 More Examples

✅ Affirmative

• I have to study.
• He has to work late.
• We have to leave now.
• They have to wear uniforms.

🚫 Negative

• I don’t have to go today.
• She doesn’t have to cook.
• We don’t have to pay.
• They don’t have to come.

❓ Questions

• Do you have to study?
• Does he have to work?
• Do we have to bring food?
• What do they have to do?

📌 Related Expressions

Must
"You must wear a mask." (stronger)
Should
"You should wear a mask." (advice)
Need to
"You need to wear a mask." (necessity)

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

Using "Have to" in Context

• I have to study for tomorrow’s test.
• She has to wake up early.
• We don’t have to work on Sunday.
• Do they have to bring their books?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ He have to go.
✅ He has to go.

❌ I has to study.
✅ I have to study.

❌ Do she have to work?
✅ Does she have to work?

❌ We doesn’t have to pay.
✅ We don’t have to pay.
