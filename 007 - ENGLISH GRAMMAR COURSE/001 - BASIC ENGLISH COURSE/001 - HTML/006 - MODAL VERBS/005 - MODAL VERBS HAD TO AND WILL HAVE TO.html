⏳ MODAL VERBS: HAD TO & WILL HAVE TO ⏳

Talking About Past and Future Obligation

🌟 What Do They Mean?

HAD TO and WILL HAVE TO are used to express obligation in the past and future. They are the past and future forms of have to.

  • HAD TO: Something was necessary in the past.
  • WILL HAVE TO: Something will be necessary in the future.

📋 Structure Comparison

Form Structure Example
Had to Subject + had to + base verb I had to study last night.
Will have to Subject + will have to + base verb She will have to work tomorrow.

💡 Quick Tip

Use had to for past obligations and will have to for future ones. Both are stronger than “should”.

🎯 Common Uses

1. Using "Had to"
To describe something necessary in the past

• I had to wake up early.
• She had to finish her homework.
• We had to cancel the trip.
• They had to wear uniforms.

2. Using "Will Have To"
To describe something necessary in the future

• I will have to study tonight.
• He will have to take the test.
• We will have to leave early.
• They will have to bring their books.

📝 More Examples

✅ HAD TO

• I had to work last weekend.
• She had to go to the doctor.
• We had to clean the classroom.
• They had to wait for the bus.

✅ WILL HAVE TO

• I will have to wake up early.
• He will have to study more.
• We will have to take the exam.
• They will have to wear masks.

📌 Related Expressions

Have to
"I have to go now." (present)
Must
"You must follow the rules." (strong obligation)
Should
"You should study." (advice)

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

Using Both in Context

• I had to cancel my plans because of the rain.
• She will have to take care of her brother tomorrow.
• We had to leave early yesterday.
• They will have to study for the final exam.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ He had to goes.
✅ He had to go.

❌ She will has to study.
✅ She will have to study.

❌ We had to went.
✅ We had to go.

❌ They will have study.
✅ They will have to study.

Use "Had to" for Past Obligation and "Will Have To" for Future Obligation!
