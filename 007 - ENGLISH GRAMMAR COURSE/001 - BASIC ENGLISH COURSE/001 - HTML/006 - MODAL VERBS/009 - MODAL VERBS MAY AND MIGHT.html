🔮 MODAL VERBS: MAY & MIGHT 🔮

Expressing Possibility and Permission

🌟 What Do "May" and "Might" Mean?

MAY and MIGHT are modal verbs used to express possibility and sometimes permission. They are often interchangeable when talking about uncertain situations.

  • MAY: Used for formal permission or possibility.
  • MIGHT: Used for possibility, often slightly less certain than "may".

📋 Structure with "May" and "Might"

Form Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + may/might + base verb She may come later.
Negative Subject + may/might not + base verb They might not join us.
Question May/Might + subject + base verb? May I ask a question?

💡 Quick Tip

Use may for formal permission and possibility. Use might for informal or uncertain possibilities.

🎯 Common Uses

1. Possibility
To say something is possible but not certain

• It may rain today.
• She might be at home.
• We may go to the beach.
• They might win the game.

2. Permission (May only)
To ask or give formal permission

• May I leave early?
• You may use your phone now.
• May we enter the room?
• Students may ask questions.

3. Polite Suggestions
To offer or suggest something gently

• You might want to check your answers.
• We may need more time.
• He might consider another option.
• She may prefer to stay home.

📝 More Examples

✅ MAY

• I may visit my grandma.
• She may be tired.
• May I open the window?
• We may arrive late.

✅ MIGHT

• I might go to the party.
• He might be sleeping.
• They might not come.
• What might happen next?

📌 Related Expressions

Can
"Can I leave?" (informal permission)
Could
"It could rain." (possibility)
Should
"You should stay home." (advice)

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

Using "May" and "Might" in Context

• I may go to the market later.
• She might be busy right now.
• May I borrow your book?
• They might not know the answer.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ He may to come.
✅ He may come.

❌ She mights be late.
✅ She might be late.

❌ May I to ask?
✅ May I ask?

❌ They might comes.
✅ They might come.
