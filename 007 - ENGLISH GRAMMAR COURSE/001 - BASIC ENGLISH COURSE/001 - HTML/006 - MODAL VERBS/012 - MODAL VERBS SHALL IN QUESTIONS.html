🗨️ MODAL VERB: SHALL IN QUESTIONS 🗨️

Making Suggestions, Offers, and Decisions

🌟 What Does "Shall" Mean in Questions?

SHALL is a modal verb used in questions to make suggestions, offers, or decisions. It is more common in British English and is considered formal or polite.

  • Shall I...? → Used to offer or suggest something.
  • Shall we...? → Used to suggest a shared action or decision.

📋 Structure of "Shall" in Questions

Form Structure Example
Suggestion Shall + I/we + base verb...? Shall we go now?
Offer Shall + I + base verb...? Shall I help you?
Decision Shall + we + base verb...? Shall we order pizza?

💡 Quick Tip

Use “Shall I” to offer help. Use “Shall we” to suggest doing something together.

🎯 Common Uses of "Shall" in Questions

1. Suggestions
To propose an idea or plan

• Shall we study together?
• Shall we take a break?
• Shall we start the meeting?
• Shall we go for a walk?

2. Offers
To offer help or assistance

• Shall I carry your bag?
• Shall I open the window?
• Shall I explain it again?
• Shall I call a taxi?

3. Decisions
To ask for agreement or make a choice

• Shall we order pizza or pasta?
• Shall we leave now or wait?
• Shall we invite them?
• Shall we continue or stop?

📌 Related Expressions

Should we
"Should we go now?" (less formal)
Can I
"Can I help you?" (informal offer)
Would you like
"Would you like me to help?" (polite offer)

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ Shall we to go?
✅ Shall we go?

❌ Shall I helps you?
✅ Shall I help you?

❌ Shall we going now?
✅ Shall we go now?

❌ Shall I to explain?
✅ Shall I explain?
