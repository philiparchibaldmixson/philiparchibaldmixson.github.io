🧩 QUESTIONS WITH LONG SUBJECTS 🧩

When the Subject of the Question Is a Phrase

🌟 What Are Long Subject Questions?

In some questions, the subject is not just one word like “who” or “what” — it’s a longer phrase that includes more detail. These are still subject questions, and they follow the same rule: no auxiliary verb is needed.

Examples:

  • Which of the students in the front row answered the question?
  • What kind of music makes you feel relaxed?
  • Which of the books on the shelf belongs to you?

📋 Structure of Long Subject Questions

Question Phrase Verb Object Example
Which of the students answered the question Which of the students answered the question?
What kind of food helps your memory What kind of food helps your memory?
Which of the books on the shelf belongs to you Which of the books on the shelf belongs to you?

💡 Quick Tip

Even with a long subject, don’t use do/does/did. The subject comes first, then the verb.

🎯 Common Patterns

1. WHICH + of + group
Used to choose from a group

• Which of the students got the highest score?
• Which of the teachers speaks French?
• Which of the options seems better?

2. WHAT KIND OF + noun
Used to ask about types or categories

• What kind of music helps you concentrate?
• What kind of movies make you cry?
• What kind of exercise improves flexibility?

3. WHO + descriptive phrase
Used to describe people in detail

• Who among your classmates speaks three languages?
• Who in your family cooks the best?
• Who from the team arrived first?

📝 More Examples

✅ Present

📌 Subject vs Object (Reminder)

Subject Question
Which of the students answered?
Object Question
Which student did you answer?
Subject Question
What kind of food helps memory?
Object Question
What kind of food do you eat?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ What kind of music does help you?
✅ What kind of music helps you?

❌ Which of the students did answer?
✅ Which of the students answered?

❌ Who among your friends does speak French?
✅ Who among your friends speaks French?
