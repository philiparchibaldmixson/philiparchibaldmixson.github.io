📌 PREPOSITIONS IN QUESTIONS 📌

How to Use Prepositions Correctly in WH-Questions

🌟 What Are Prepositions in Questions?

Prepositions are words like in, on, at, for, to, with, about that show relationships between words. In questions, they often appear at the end or before the question word, depending on the formality.

Examples:

  • Who are you talking to?
  • What is she afraid of?
  • To whom did you send the email? (formal)

📋 Structure of Questions with Prepositions

Form Structure Example
Informal WH-word + auxiliary + subject + verb + preposition Who are you waiting for?
Formal Preposition + WH-word + auxiliary + subject + verb For whom are you waiting?
Embedded WH-word + verb + preposition + object What does it depend on?

💡 Quick Tip

In everyday English, it’s common to place the preposition at the end of the question. Formal English places it before the question word.

🎯 Common Prepositions in Questions

1. TO
Used with verbs like talk, listen, belong

• Who are you talking to?
• Who does this book belong to?
• To whom did you speak?

2. FOR
Used with wait, look, do

• Who are you waiting for?
• What is this gift for?
• For whom is this intended?

3. WITH
Used with go, play, work

• Who are you going with?
• Who did you play with?
• With whom did you work?

4. ABOUT / OF
Used with think, dream, be afraid

• What are you thinking about?
• What is she afraid of?
• About what are you dreaming?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ Who you are talking to?
✅ Who are you talking to?

❌ To who are you speaking?
✅ To whom are you speaking?

❌ What you are afraid of?
✅ What are you afraid of?
