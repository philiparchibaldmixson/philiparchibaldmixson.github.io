🚫 NOT vs NO 🚫

Understanding the Difference Between “NOT” and “NO”

🌟 What’s the Difference?

NOT is used to make verbs, adjectives, or adverbs negative. It’s usually placed before a verb or adjective.

NO is used before a noun to mean “zero” or “none.” It replaces “not any.”

Examples:

  • I’m not tired.
  • There is no milk in the fridge.
  • She does not like coffee.
  • We have no time left.

📋 Structure Comparison

Word Used With Example
NOT Verbs, adjectives, adverbs She is not ready.
NO Nouns (without “a” or “any”) There is no sugar.
NOT Auxiliary verbs He does not understand.
NO Before noun phrases No students were late.

💡 Quick Tip

Use NOT with verbs and adjectives. Use NO before nouns. Never use both together!

🎯 Common Uses

1. NOT + Verb
To make a verb negative

• I do not like tea.
• She is not coming.
• They have not finished.

2. NO + Noun
To say “none” or “zero”

• There is no water.
• We have no idea.
• No students were absent.

3. NOT + Adjective
To describe something negatively

• He is not happy.
• This is not easy.
• I’m not ready.

4. NO + Compound Noun
To emphasize absence

• There is no internet connection.
• We have no homework today.
• No parking allowed.

📝 More Examples

✅ Using NOT

• I’m not hungry.
• He does not want to go.
• We are not ready yet.

✅ Using NO

• No children were hurt.
• We have no plans today.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ I don’t have no money.
✅ I don’t have any money.

❌ She no is here.
✅ She is not here.

❌ We have not time.
✅ We have no time.

❌ There isn’t no food.
✅ There isn’t any food.

Use NOT to make verbs and adjectives negative. Use NO before nouns to mean “none.”

Practice with both to sound natural and accurate in English!
