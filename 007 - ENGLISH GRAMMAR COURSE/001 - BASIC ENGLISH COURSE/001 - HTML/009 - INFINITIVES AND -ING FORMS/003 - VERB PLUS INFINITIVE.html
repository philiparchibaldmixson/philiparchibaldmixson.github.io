📚 VERB + INFINITIVE 📚

Using “to” + Verb After Certain Verbs

🌟 What Is Verb + Infinitive?

Some verbs in English are followed by an infinitive — that means “to” + base verb. These combinations express actions, plans, desires, and intentions.

Examples:

  • I want to learn English.
  • She decided to stay home.
  • We hope to travel soon.

📋 Structure of Verb + Infinitive

Main Verb Infinitive Full Sentence
want to eat I want to eat now.
need to study You need to study more.
plan to travel We plan to travel next year.
decide to stay She decided to stay home.

💡 Quick Tip

Use “to” + verb after verbs like want, need, hope, plan, decide, learn, offer, promise, and forget.

🎯 Common Verb + Infinitive Combinations

1. WANT / NEED / HOPE
Desire or necessity

• I want to sleep.
• She needs to rest.
• They hope to win.

2. PLAN / DECIDE / EXPECT
Intentions

• We plan to visit Bahia.
• He decided to stay.
• I expect to finish soon.

3. OFFER / PROMISE / REFUSE
Commitments

• She offered to help.
• He promised to call.
• They refused to leave.

4. LEARN / TRY / FORGET
Actions and efforts

• I’m learning to cook.
• We’ll try to fix it.
• Don’t forget to lock the door.

📌 Verb + Infinitive vs Verb + Gerund

Infinitive
I want to learn.
Gerund
I enjoy learning.
Infinitive
She needs to rest.
Gerund
She likes resting.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong → ✅ Correct

❌ I want learning.
✅ I want to learn.

❌ She needs go.
✅ She needs to go.

❌ We hope pass.
✅ We hope to pass.

❌ He decided stay.
✅ He decided to stay.
