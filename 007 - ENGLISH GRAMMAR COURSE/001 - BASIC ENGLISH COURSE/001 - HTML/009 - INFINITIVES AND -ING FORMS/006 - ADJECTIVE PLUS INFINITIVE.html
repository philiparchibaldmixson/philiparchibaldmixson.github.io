😊 ADJECTIVE + INFINITIVE 😊

Using Adjectives Followed by “to” + Verb

🌟 What Is Adjective + Infinitive?

We often use an adjective followed by an infinitive (to + base verb) to express feelings, opinions, or reactions about actions. This structure is common and natural in English.

Examples:

  • She is happy to help.
  • It’s difficult to explain.
  • He was surprised to see us.

📋 Structure of Adjective + Infinitive

Subject Be Verb Adjective Infinitive Full Sentence
She is happy to help She is happy to help.
It is hard to understand It is hard to understand.
They were excited to travel They were excited to travel.

💡 Quick Tip

Use adjective + “to” + verb to describe how someone feels or reacts to an action.

🎯 Common Adjectives Used with Infinitives

1. HAPPY / GLAD / EXCITED
Positive emotions

• I’m happy to help.
• She’s glad to see you.
• They’re excited to travel.

2. SAD / AFRAID / SURPRISED
Negative or mixed emotions

• He’s sad to leave.
• I’m afraid to ask.
• She was surprised to hear the news.

3. EASY / HARD / DIFFICULT
Describing difficulty

• It’s easy to learn.
• It’s hard to explain.
• It’s difficult to hear.

4. NICE / FUN / GOOD
Describing experience

• It’s nice to meet you.
• It’s fun to play soccer.
• It’s good to relax.

📌 Compare with Other Infinitive Structures

Form Example
Adjective + Infinitive She is happy to help.
It + Adjective + Infinitive It is important to study.
Verb + Infinitive I want to learn.
Verb + Object + Infinitive She told me to study.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

• I’m glad to help with your homework.
• She’s afraid to speak in public.
• It’s easy to learn with practice.
• He was surprised to see the results.
• We’re excited to start the project.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ She happy to help.
✅ She is happy to help.

❌ I glad to see you.
✅ I am glad to see you.

❌ It easy to learn.
✅ It is easy to learn.

❌ They excited to travel.
✅ They are excited to travel.
