💪 ADJECTIVE + ENOUGH + TO + VERB 💪

Using “enough” with adjectives and infinitives

🌟 What Is This Structure?

We use adjective + enough + to + verb to say that someone or something has the necessary quality to do an action. It shows that the condition is sufficient.

  • She is strong enough to lift the box.
  • He is smart enough to solve the problem.
  • We are ready enough to start now.

📋 Structure

Subject Be Verb Adjective Enough Infinitive Full Sentence
She is strong enough to lift She is strong enough to lift it.
He was fast enough to win He was fast enough to win the race.
They are quiet enough to concentrate They are quiet enough to concentrate.

💡 Quick Tip

Use this pattern to show that someone or something meets the condition to do something.

🎯 Common Adjectives Used with “Enough”

  • Physical: strong enough to carry, tall enough to reach, fast enough to win
  • Mental: smart enough to solve, ready enough to begin, good enough to play
  • Situational: quiet enough to sleep, safe enough to cross, clear enough to follow
  • Emotional: brave enough to speak, confident enough to try, calm enough to explain

📌 Compare with “Too + Adjective + to”

Form Meaning Example
Adjective + enough + to Can do it She is strong enough to lift it.
Too + adjective + to Cannot do it She is too weak to lift it.
Adjective + enough + to Possible It’s clear enough to follow.
Too + adjective + to Not possible It’s too confusing to follow.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ She enough strong to lift.
✅ She is strong enough to lift.

❌ He is enough smart to solve.
✅ He is smart enough to solve.

❌ We ready enough start.
✅ We are ready enough to start.

❌ It quiet enough sleep.
✅ It is quiet enough to sleep.
