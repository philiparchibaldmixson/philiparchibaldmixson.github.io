🚀 PHRASAL VERBS 🚀

Common Verb + Particle Combinations in English

🌟 What Are Phrasal Verbs?

A phrasal verb is a combination of a verb + a particle (preposition or adverb) that creates a new meaning. These are very common in everyday English and often have idiomatic meanings.

  • She gave up smoking. (quit)
  • I ran into an old friend. (met unexpectedly)
  • They look after the baby. (take care of)

📋 Structure

Phrasal Verb Meaning Example
give up quit She gave up chocolate.
look after take care of I look after my little brother.
run into meet by chance We ran into our teacher at the mall.
turn on activate Please turn on the lights.
pick up collect Can you pick up the kids?

💡 Quick Tip

Some phrasal verbs are separable (you can put the object in the middle), and others are not. Learn them in context!

🎯 Common Phrasal Verbs

  • give up → He gave up trying.
  • look after → She looks after her dog.
  • run into → I ran into my cousin.
  • turn on → He turned on the TV.
  • pick up → We picked up groceries.
  • find out → She found out the truth.
  • take off → The plane took off at 6.
  • put off → They put off the meeting.
  • get along → We get along well.

📌 Separable vs Inseparable

Type Example
Separable He turned off the light / He turned the light off.
Inseparable We looked after the baby. (❌ We looked the baby after.)
Separable She picked up the phone / She picked the phone up.
Inseparable I ran into my friend. (❌ I ran my friend into.)

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • I gave up sugar last year.
  • She looked after her neighbor’s cat.
  • We ran into traffic on the way.
  • He turned off the computer.
  • They picked up their kids from school.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ I gave smoking.
✅ I gave up smoking.

❌ She looked the baby after.
✅ She looked after the baby.

❌ We picked the kids.
✅ We picked up the kids.

❌ He turned the TV.
✅ He turned on the TV.
