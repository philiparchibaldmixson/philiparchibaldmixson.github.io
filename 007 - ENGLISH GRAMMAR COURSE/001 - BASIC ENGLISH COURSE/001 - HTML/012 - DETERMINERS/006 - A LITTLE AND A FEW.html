🥄 DETERMINERS: A LITTLE & A FEW 🥄

Talking About Small Quantities in English

🌟 What Do They Mean?

A little and a few are used to talk about small amounts. They are both positive and suggest that there is some quantity — not zero.

  • A little → used with uncountable nouns
  • A few → used with countable nouns

📋 Structure

Determiner Used With Example
A little Uncountable nouns I have a little money.
A few Countable nouns She has a few friends.

💡 Quick Tip

Use “a little” for things you can’t count (like water, time, sugar). Use “a few” for things you can count (like books, people, chairs).

🎯 Common Examples

✅ Using “A Little”

  • I need a little help.
  • She drank a little juice.
  • We have a little time left.
  • He added a little sugar.
  • There’s a little noise outside.

✅ Using “A Few”

  • I saw a few birds in the tree.
  • She has a few questions.
  • We invited a few friends.
  • He bought a few apples.
  • There are a few chairs available.

📝 More Sentences

✅ A Little

• I have a little experience with computers.
• We need a little more time.
• She gave me a little advice.

✅ A Few

• I read a few pages before bed.
• They asked a few questions.
• We saw a few stars in the sky.

📌 Compare: A Little vs A Few

A Little (Uncountable) A Few (Countable)
We have a little milk. We have a few bottles of milk.
She needs a little rest. She needs a few pillows.
I have a little information. I have a few facts.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • We need a little oil for the recipe.
  • He brought a few tools to fix the bike.
  • I have a little free time this afternoon.
  • She made a few mistakes, but nothing serious.
  • There’s a little hope left.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ I have a few money.
✅ I have a little money.

❌ She drank a few water.
✅ She drank a little water.

❌ We saw a little birds.
✅ We saw a few birds.

❌ He has a little friends.
✅ He has a few friends.

Use “a little” for uncountable nouns and “a few” for countable nouns!
