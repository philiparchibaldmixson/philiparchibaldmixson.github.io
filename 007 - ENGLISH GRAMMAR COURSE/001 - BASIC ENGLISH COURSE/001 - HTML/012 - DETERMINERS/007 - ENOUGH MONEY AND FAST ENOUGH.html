Using “Enough” to Talk About Quantity and Ability

🌟 What Does “Enough” Mean?

Enough means the right amount or sufficient. It can be used as a determiner before nouns or as an adverb after adjectives/adverbs.

  • Enough + noun → We have enough money.
  • Adjective/adverb + enough → He isn’t fast enough.

📋 Structure

Form Position Example
Enough + noun Before the noun We have enough chairs.
Adjective + enough After the adjective She is strong enough.
Adverb + enough After the adverb He ran quickly enough.

💡 Quick Tip

Use “enough” before nouns, but after adjectives and adverbs. Watch the position!

🎯 Common Examples

✅ Enough + Noun

  • We have enough food.
  • Do you have enough money?
  • There aren’t enough chairs.
  • She brought enough clothes.
  • He didn’t have enough time.

✅ Adjective + Enough

  • She is tall enough to reach the shelf.
  • He isn’t strong enough to lift it.
  • Are you ready enough for the test?
  • This isn’t clear enough.
  • It’s cold enough to wear a jacket.

✅ Adverb + Enough

  • He ran fast enough to win.
  • She spoke loudly enough for everyone to hear.
  • Did you study hard enough?
  • They arrived early enough.
  • We didn’t walk quickly enough.

📝 More Sentences

✅ Enough + Noun

• I don’t have enough energy today.
• We need enough space for everyone.
• She didn’t bring enough water.

✅ Adjective/Adverb + Enough

• He isn’t fast enough to catch the bus.
• Are you confident enough to speak?
• She didn’t sing loudly enough.

📌 Compare: Enough Money vs Fast Enough

Enough + Noun Adjective/Adverb + Enough
We have enough money to buy it. He isn’t fast enough to win.
Do you have enough time? She isn’t strong enough.
They brought enough food. We didn’t arrive early enough.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • I didn’t have enough money to pay.
  • She wasn’t fast enough to catch the train.
  • We had enough chairs for everyone.
  • He didn’t speak clearly enough.
  • Do you have enough time to finish?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ He has enough fast to win.
✅ He is fast enough to win.

❌ I have money enough.
✅ I have enough money.

❌ She didn’t loud enough speak.
✅ She didn’t speak loudly enough.

❌ We have chairs enough.
✅ We have enough chairs.

Use “enough” before nouns and after adjectives/adverbs to express sufficient quantity or ability!
