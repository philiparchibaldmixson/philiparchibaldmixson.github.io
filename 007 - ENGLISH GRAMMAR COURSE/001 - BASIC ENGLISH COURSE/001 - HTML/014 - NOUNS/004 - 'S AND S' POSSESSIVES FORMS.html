📌 POSSESSIVE FORM: ’S & S’ 📌

Showing Ownership in English

🌟 What Is the Possessive Form?

We use ’s and s’ to show that something belongs to someone or something. It’s called the possessive form.

  • ’s → for singular nouns (e.g., Anna’s phone)
  • s’ → for plural nouns ending in “s” (e.g., the teachers’ lounge)

📋 Structure

Form Used With Example
noun + ’s Singular noun John’s car is red.
noun + s’ Plural noun ending in “s” The students’ books are on the table.
irregular plural + ’s Plural noun not ending in “s” The children’s toys are everywhere.
compound noun + ’s Two or more words My brother-in-law’s car is fast.

💡 Quick Tip

Use ’s for singular nouns and irregular plurals. Use s’ for regular plurals ending in “s”.

🎯 Common Examples

✅ ’S (Singular)

  • Maria’s phone is ringing.
  • This is the dog’s toy.
  • My friend’s house is big.
  • The teacher’s desk is clean.
  • James’s book is on the shelf.

✅ S’ (Plural)

  • The girls’ room is upstairs.
  • All the players’ uniforms are ready.
  • The dogs’ bowls are empty.
  • The teachers’ meeting is at 3 PM.
  • The parents’ opinions matter.

✅ Irregular Plural + ’S

  • The men’s jackets are here.
  • The women’s team won.
  • The children’s playground is open.
  • The people’s voices were heard.

📝 More Sentences

• That is my sister’s car.
• The students’ homework is due tomorrow.
• We visited the children’s museum.
• Tom’s idea was brilliant.
• The cats’ toys are everywhere.

📌 Compare: ’S vs S’

’S (Singular) S’ (Plural)
Anna’s book The girls’ books
The dog’s collar The dogs’ collars
My friend’s idea My friends’ ideas

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • This is my mom’s recipe.
  • The students’ answers were correct.
  • Peter’s phone is new.
  • The dogs’ park is nearby.
  • The children’s books are on the shelf.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ The girls’s room
✅ The girls’ room

❌ The childrens’ toys
✅ The children’s toys

❌ The teachers desk
✅ The teacher’s desk

❌ The cats toy
✅ The cat’s toy

Use ’s for singular nouns and irregular plurals. Use s’ for regular plurals ending in “s”!
