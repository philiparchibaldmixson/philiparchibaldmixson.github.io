🔗 RELATIVE PRONOUN: THAT 🔗

Connecting People and Things with “That”

🌟 What Does “That” Do?

The relative pronoun that is used to connect a noun to a relative clause. It can refer to people, things, or animals and is often used in defining (essential) relative clauses.

  • Use that instead of “who” or “which” in defining clauses.
  • Do not use commas before “that”.

📋 Structure

Used For Example
People The student that got the highest grade is here.
Things This is the book that I told you about.
Animals The dog that barked all night is mine.

💡 Quick Tip

Use “that” in essential information clauses. If the clause is necessary to understand the noun, “that” is perfect!

🎯 Common Examples

  • The phone that I lost was new.
  • The man that helped me was very kind.
  • The movie that we watched was exciting.
  • The car that she drives is electric.
  • The teacher that teaches English is from Canada.

📝 More Sentences

• The cake that she made was delicious.
• I know a place that serves great pizza.
• The dog that lives next door is friendly.
• The student that answered first was correct.
• The shoes that I bought are too small.

📌 Compare: WHO / WHICH vs THAT

WHO / WHICH THAT
The woman who called is my aunt. The woman that called is my aunt.
The book which you gave me is great. The book that you gave me is great.
The dog which barked is gone. The dog that barked is gone.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • This is the song that I love.
  • The person that called you is my friend.
  • He wore the jacket that he bought in Paris.
  • The house that we visited was beautiful.
  • The idea that she suggested was brilliant.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ The man, that helped me, was kind.
✅ The man that helped me was kind.

❌ The book that, I read, was boring.
✅ The book that I read was boring.

❌ I saw a cat that it was sleeping.
✅ I saw a cat that was sleeping.

❌ She’s the girl that she won.
✅ She’s the girl that won.

Use “that” to connect essential information about people, things, or animals. It keeps your sentences clear and direct!
