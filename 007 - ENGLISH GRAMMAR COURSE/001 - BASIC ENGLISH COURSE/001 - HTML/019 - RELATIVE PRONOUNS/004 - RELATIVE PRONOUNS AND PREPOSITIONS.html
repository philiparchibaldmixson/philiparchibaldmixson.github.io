📎 RELATIVE PRONOUNS + PREPOSITIONS 📎

Connecting Clauses with “who”, “which”, “that” + prepositions

🌟 What’s the Rule?

When a relative clause includes a preposition (like to, with, about, for), it can appear in two places:

  • ✅ at the end of the clause → informal and common
  • ✅ before the relative pronoun → formal style

We use who, which, that with prepositions to give more information about people or things.

📋 Structure

Style Form Example
Informal noun + relative pronoun + verb + preposition The person who I spoke to was helpful.
Formal noun + preposition + relative pronoun The person to whom I spoke was helpful.
Thing (informal) noun + which/that + verb + preposition The book which I talked about is here.
Thing (formal) noun + preposition + which The book about which I talked is here.

💡 Quick Tip

In spoken English, we usually put the preposition at the end. In writing, formal style puts it before the pronoun.

🎯 Common Examples

✅ People

  • The woman who I work with is very smart.
  • The man to whom I sent the email hasn’t replied.
  • The student who I spoke to was confused.
  • The person for whom I baked the cake was happy.

✅ Things

  • The movie which I talked about is on Netflix.
  • The chair on which I sat was broken.
  • The phone that I paid for is expensive.
  • The topic about which we spoke was interesting.

📝 More Sentences

• The person who I borrowed it from is my friend.
• The person from whom I borrowed it is my friend.
• The company which I applied to is hiring.
• The company to which I applied is hiring.
• The book that I was looking for is here.
• The book for which I was looking is here.

📌 Compare: Informal vs Formal

Informal Formal
The person who I spoke to was kind. The person to whom I spoke was kind.
The book which I talked about is here. The book about which I talked is here.
The friend who I traveled with is Brazilian. The friend with whom I traveled is Brazilian.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • The person who I sent the message to hasn’t replied.
  • The person to whom I sent the message hasn’t replied.
  • The project which I worked on is finished.
  • The project on which I worked is finished.
  • The friend who I shared the room with was awesome.
  • The friend with whom I shared the room was awesome.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ The person to I spoke was nice.
✅ The person to whom I spoke was nice.

❌ The book which I talked was boring.
✅ The book which I talked about was boring.

❌ The girl with I danced was friendly.
✅ The girl with whom I danced was friendly.

❌ The man who I gave the gift was happy.
✅ The man who I gave the gift to was happy.

Use prepositions with relative pronouns to describe people and things clearly. Choose formal or informal style depending on context!
