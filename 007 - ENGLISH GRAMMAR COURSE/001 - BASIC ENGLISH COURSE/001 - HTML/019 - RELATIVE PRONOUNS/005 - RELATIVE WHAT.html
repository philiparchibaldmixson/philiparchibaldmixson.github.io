🔍 RELATIVE PRONOUN: WHAT 🔍

Using “what” to mean “the thing that”

🌟 What Does “What” Mean?

The relative pronoun what means “the thing that”. It introduces a clause that acts as a noun and refers to something unknown, unspecified, or general.

  • I didn’t understand what she said. → “the thing that she said”
  • What he did was amazing. → “The thing that he did…”

📋 Structure

Form Example
what + subject + verb What she said surprised me.
what + verb + object I didn’t hear what he said.
what + be + complement What I need is a break.

💡 Quick Tip

“What” is not followed by a noun. It already includes the noun idea: “the thing that”.

🎯 Common Examples

  • What you said is true.
  • I don’t know what he wants.
  • What she did was brave.
  • Tell me what you need.
  • What happened yesterday was strange.

📝 More Sentences

• What I saw shocked me.
• He explained what he meant.
• What they built was impressive.
• I didn’t understand what she was talking about.
• What matters is your effort.

📌 Compare: WHAT vs THAT / WHICH

WHAT (unspecified) THAT / WHICH (specific)
I didn’t hear what she said. The words that she said were unclear.
What he did was wrong. The action which he took was wrong.
What I need is rest. The thing that I need is rest.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • What you decide is up to you.
  • I didn’t understand what they meant.
  • What we discussed was confidential.
  • What she wants is respect.
  • What I love most is traveling.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ I know the what he said.
✅ I know what he said.

❌ What book you read was interesting.
✅ What you read was interesting.

❌ I didn’t understand that what she meant.
✅ I didn’t understand what she meant.

❌ The what he did was wrong.
✅ What he did was wrong.

Use “what” to refer to unknown or general things. It means “the thing that” and adds clarity!
