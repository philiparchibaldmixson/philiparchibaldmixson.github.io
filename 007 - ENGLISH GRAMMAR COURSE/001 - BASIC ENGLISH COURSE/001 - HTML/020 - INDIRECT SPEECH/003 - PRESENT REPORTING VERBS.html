🗣️ INDIRECT SPEECH: PRESENT REPORTING VERBS 🗣️

Using “say”, “tell”, “ask” in the Present

🌟 What Does It Mean?

When we report speech using a present tense reporting verb (like say, tell, ask), the original verb tense usually stays the same. This is common in news, storytelling, or when the information is still true.

  • She says she likes chocolate. ✅
  • He tells me he is tired. ✅

📋 Structure

Reporting Verb Form Example
say say + (that) + clause She says (that) she is happy.
tell tell + object + (that) + clause He tells me (that) he feels sick.
ask ask + object + if / question word + clause They ask me if I know the answer.

💡 Quick Tip

Use present reporting verbs when the information is still true or relevant now.

🎯 Common Examples

  • She says she lives in London.
  • He tells me he has a dog.
  • They say they are ready.
  • My teacher tells us English is important.
  • She asks if I like pizza.

📝 More Sentences

• He says he feels better today.
• She tells me she has finished the homework.
• They say they don’t understand the question.
• My friend asks what time the movie starts.
• He asks if I want to join them.

📌 Compare: Past vs Present Reporting Verb

Past Reporting Verb Present Reporting Verb
She said she was tired. She says she is tired.
He told me he had a car. He tells me he has a car.
They asked if I was busy. They ask if I am busy.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • My mom says dinner is ready.
  • The teacher tells us the test starts at 9.
  • He says he doesn’t like coffee.
  • She asks if I can help her.
  • They say the weather is nice today.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ She says she was tired. (if still true)
✅ She says she is tired.

❌ He tells me he had a dog. (if still true)
✅ He tells me he has a dog.

❌ They ask if I knew the answer.
✅ They ask if I know the answer.

❌ She says she didn’t understand.
✅ She says she doesn’t understand.

Use present reporting verbs when the information is still true or current. Keep the original tense!
