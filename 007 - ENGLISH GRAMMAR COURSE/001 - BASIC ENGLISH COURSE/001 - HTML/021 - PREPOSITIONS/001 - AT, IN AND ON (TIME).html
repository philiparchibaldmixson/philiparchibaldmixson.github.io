🕒 PREPOSITIONS OF TIME: AT • IN • ON 🕒

Using “at”, “in” and “on” to talk about time

🌟 What Are Prepositions of Time?

We use at, in and on to talk about specific points, periods and days in time. Each one has a different use:

  • at → specific times
  • on → specific days and dates
  • in → longer periods (months, years, centuries)

📋 Structure

Preposition Used For Example
at Specific time at 7:00 / at midnight / at lunchtime
on Specific day or date on Monday / on July 4th / on my birthday
in Longer period in June / in 2025 / in the morning

💡 Quick Tip

Use “at” for points, “on” for surfaces (dates), and “in” for containers (months, years, parts of day).

🎯 Common Examples

✅ AT

  • at 6 o’clock
  • at noon
  • at bedtime
  • at midnight
  • at the moment

✅ ON

  • on Monday
  • on Christmas Day
  • on July 4th
  • on my birthday
  • on the weekend (US)

✅ IN

  • in the morning
  • in June
  • in 2025
  • in the 21st century
  • in the summer

📝 More Sentences

• I wake up at 7 a.m.
• We met on Friday.
• The concert is on May 12th.
• She was born in 1990.
• We go on vacation in December.
• The meeting starts at noon.
• I love walking in the evening.

📌 Compare: AT vs IN vs ON

AT ON IN
at 5:00 on Monday in June
at midnight on July 4th in 2025
at lunchtime on my birthday in the morning

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • The train leaves at 8:30.
  • We’ll meet on Saturday.
  • My birthday is on October 10th.
  • I was born in 2001.
  • She likes jogging in the morning.
  • The show starts at noon.
  • We travel in the summer.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ I was born at 1990.
✅ I was born in 1990.

❌ The party is in Friday.
✅ The party is on Friday.

❌ We’ll meet in 5 o’clock.
✅ We’ll meet at 5 o’clock.

❌ I go to school on the morning.
✅ I go to school in the morning.

Use “at” for specific times, “on” for days and dates, and “in” for longer periods!
