⏳ PREPOSITIONS: FROM ... TO • UNTIL • BY ⏳

Talking About Time and Deadlines

🌟 What Do They Mean?

We use from ... to, until, and by to talk about time periods, limits, and deadlines.

  • from ... to → shows the beginning and end of a period
  • until → shows how long something continues
  • by → shows a deadline (no later than)

📋 Structure

Preposition Used For Example
from ... to Start and end of a time period I work from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
until Continuing up to a point in time We stayed until midnight.
by Deadline (no later than) Please finish by Friday.

💡 Quick Tip

Use from ... to for duration, until for continuation, and by for deadlines.

🎯 Common Examples

✅ FROM ... TO

  • I studied from 8 a.m. to 12 p.m.
  • The store is open from Monday to Saturday.
  • We’ll be on vacation from June to August.

✅ UNTIL

  • She waited until he arrived.
  • The movie lasts until 10 p.m.
  • I’ll stay until the end.

✅ BY

  • You must submit the form by Monday.
  • I’ll be home by 6 o’clock.
  • Finish your homework by tomorrow.

📝 More Sentences

• The meeting is from 2 to 4 p.m.
• We stayed until the rain stopped.
• Please return the book by next week.
• I’ll be working from home until Friday.
• The offer is valid until midnight.
• You need to pay by the 10th.

📌 Compare: FROM ... TO vs UNTIL vs BY

FROM ... TO UNTIL BY
We studied from 9 to 11. We studied until 11. We had to finish by 11.
The store is open from Monday to Friday. The store is open until Friday. Submit your order by Friday.
I’ll be away from June to August. I’ll be away until August. I’ll return by August.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • We’re open from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  • She worked until she graduated.
  • Please send the email by noon.
  • I’ll be in class until 3 p.m.
  • The event runs from Friday to Sunday.
  • You must register by the deadline.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ I’ll be here from 5.
✅ I’ll be here from 5 to 7.

❌ She stayed by midnight.
✅ She stayed until midnight.

❌ Finish this until Friday.
✅ Finish this by Friday.

❌ I’ll wait by you arrive.
✅ I’ll wait until you arrive.

Use “from ... to” for duration, “until” for continuation, and “by” for deadlines. Mastering these helps you express time clearly!
