⏱️ PREPOSITIONS: FOR • DURING • WHILE ⏱️

Talking About Duration and Time Periods

🌟 What Do They Mean?

We use for, during and while to talk about time periods and actions happening in time. Each one has a different use:

  • for → duration (how long)
  • during → when something happens inside a time/event
  • while → two actions happening at the same time

📋 Structure

Word Used For Example
for Length of time I slept for 8 hours.
during Inside a time/event I ate popcorn during the movie.
while Two actions at the same time I listened to music while studying.

💡 Quick Tip

Use “for” with time amounts, “during” with events, and “while” with full actions (subject + verb).

🎯 Common Examples

✅ FOR

  • I stayed for two weeks.
  • She worked for five hours.
  • We talked for a long time.

✅ DURING

  • I fell asleep during the class.
  • She cried during the movie.
  • We had fun during the trip.

✅ WHILE

  • I cooked while she cleaned.
  • He listened while I explained.
  • They danced while the music played.

📝 More Sentences

• I waited for 30 minutes.
• She studied for three hours.
• He laughed during the show.
• We got tired during the hike.
• I was reading while he was watching TV.
• She was singing while I played guitar.

📌 Compare: FOR vs DURING vs WHILE

FOR DURING WHILE
I slept for 8 hours. I slept during the flight. I slept while she was driving.
We talked for 20 minutes. We talked during lunch. We talked while eating lunch.
He worked for 5 hours. He worked during the afternoon. He worked while I rested.

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • I waited for the bus.
  • She got sick during the trip.
  • We laughed while watching the video.
  • He studied for the exam.
  • I was tired during the meeting.
  • They talked while walking.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ I waited during 5 minutes.
✅ I waited for 5 minutes.

❌ She slept for the movie.
✅ She slept during the movie.

❌ I listened music during studying.
✅ I listened to music while studying.

❌ He worked while 5 hours.
✅ He worked for 5 hours.

Use “for” with durations, “during” with events, and “while” with simultaneous actions. Each one helps you express time clearly!
