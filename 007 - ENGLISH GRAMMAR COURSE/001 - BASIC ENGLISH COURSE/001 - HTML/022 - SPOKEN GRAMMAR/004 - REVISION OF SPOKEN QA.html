🗣️ SPOKEN GRAMMAR: QUESTION & ANSWER REVISION 🗣️

Sound Natural When Asking and Answering

🌟 What Is Spoken Grammar?

Spoken grammar is how we use grammar naturally in conversation. It’s often simpler, faster, and more flexible than written grammar. Let’s review how we ask and answer questions in real life!

  • Use contractions and short forms
  • Use short answers and reply questions
  • Use rising or falling intonation
  • Drop auxiliary verbs in casual speech (sometimes)

📋 Question Types

Type Example Spoken Tip
Yes/No Do you like it? Use rising intonation
Wh- Where do you live? Use falling intonation
Tag It’s nice, isn’t it? Use falling for confirmation
Reply She’s coming. → Is she? Use to show interest

💡 Quick Tip

In spoken English, we often skip words or use short forms to sound more natural.

🎯 Common Short Answers

  • Yes, I do. / No, I don’t.
  • Yes, she is. / No, she isn’t.
  • Yes, they did. / No, they didn’t.
  • Yes, we have. / No, we haven’t.
  • Yes, he will. / No, he won’t.
  • Yes, I can. / No, I can’t.

📝 Reply Questions

• I love chocolate. → Do you?
• She’s coming later. → Is she?
• They didn’t call. → Did they?
• We’ve finished. → Have you?
• He can drive. → Can he?
• She won’t be late. → Won’t she?

📌 Spoken vs Written

Spoken Written
You coming? Are you coming?
She’s here, isn’t she? She is here, isn’t she?
He didn’t call, did he? He did not call, did he?
We’ve met, haven’t we? We have met, haven’t we?

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • Do you live here? → Yes, I do.
  • She’s your sister, isn’t she?
  • I work in Itamaraju. → Do you?
  • They’ve arrived. → Have they?
  • Can you help me? → No, I can’t.
  • We’re going now. → Are you?

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ You are coming, isn’t you?
✅ You are coming, aren’t you?

❌ She don’t like it, do she?
✅ She doesn’t like it, does she?

❌ Yes, I am do.
✅ Yes, I do.

❌ I like pizza. Is you?
✅ I like pizza. Do you?

Spoken grammar is flexible and natural. Use short answers, reply questions, and tags to sound fluent!
