🗣️ SPOKEN GRAMMAR: LEAVING OUT WORDS 🗣️

Speaking Naturally and Efficiently

🌟 What Does It Mean?

In spoken English, we often leave out words to sound more natural, casual, and fluent. This is called ellipsis. Native speakers do it all the time — especially in questions, answers, and everyday phrases.

  • We drop auxiliary verbs (Are, Do, Have)
  • We skip subjects (I, You, We)
  • We shorten phrases to the key words

📋 Structure

Full Sentence Spoken Version
Are you coming? You coming?
Do you want some? Want some?
Have you finished? Finished?
I don’t know. Dunno.
Let me see. Lemme see.
Give me that. Gimme that.

💡 Quick Tip

Leaving out words is common in casual speech — but not in formal writing!

🎯 Common Examples

  • You okay? (Are you okay?)
  • Coming? (Are you coming?)
  • Need help? (Do you need help?)
  • Seen it? (Have you seen it?)
  • Got it? (Have you got it?)
  • Busy? (Are you busy?)
  • Home? (Are you home?)

📌 Spoken vs Written

Spoken Written
You coming? Are you coming?
Want some? Do you want some?
Finished? Have you finished?
He here? Is he here?
Need help? Do you need help?

🔄 Real-Life Sentences

  • You ready? (Are you ready?)
  • She coming? (Is she coming?)
  • We meeting later? (Are we meeting later?)
  • He working today? (Is he working today?)
  • Need a ride? (Do you need a ride?)
  • Got everything? (Have you got everything?)

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ You are coming?
✅ You coming?

❌ Do want some?
✅ Want some?

❌ Have finished?
✅ Finished?

❌ Is she is here?
✅ She here?

Leaving out words helps you sound fluent and natural in conversation. Just keep it clear!
