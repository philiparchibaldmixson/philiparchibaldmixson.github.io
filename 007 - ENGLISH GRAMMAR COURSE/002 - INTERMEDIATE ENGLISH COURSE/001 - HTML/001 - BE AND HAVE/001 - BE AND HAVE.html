📚 BE, HAVE & THERE IS 📚

Master the Most Essential Verbs and Structures

🌟 The Verb BE - Complete Guide

The verb BE is the most important verb in English. It is used to describe states, conditions, identities, and locations. Unlike other verbs, BE changes form depending on the subject and tense. It is also an irregular verb, which means it doesn't follow regular patterns.

Present Simple Forms:

Subject Full Form Contraction Example
I am I'm I am happy / I'm happy
You are You're You are tall / You're tall
He/She/It is He's/She's/It's She is smart / She's smart
We are We're We are students / We're students
They are They're They are friends / They're friends
📝 When to Use BE:
Identity:
I am a teacher. / She is Brazilian.
Description:
The car is red. / They are tired.
Location:
We are at home. / The book is on the table.
Age:
I am 25 years old. / She is thirty.

🔄 BE - Negative and Questions

Negative Forms:

Subject Full Negative Contraction
I am not I'm not
You are not You're not / You aren't
He/She/It is not He's not / He isn't
We are not We're not / We aren't
They are not They're not / They aren't

Question Forms:

Yes/No Questions - Invert BE and Subject:
  • Am I late? → Yes, you are. / No, you aren't.
  • Are you ready? → Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
  • Is she at home? → Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
  • Are they doctors? → Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.
WH- Questions:
  • Where are you? → I'm at the park.
  • What is your name? → My name is Maria.
  • How are you? → I'm fine, thank you.
  • Who is that? → That's my brother.

💼 The Verb HAVE - Complete Guide

The verb HAVE is used to show possession, relationships, experiences, and obligations. Like BE, it changes form depending on the subject.

Present Simple Forms:

Subject Full Form Contraction Example
I/You/We/They have I've/You've/We've/They've I have a car / I've got a car
He/She/It has He's/She's/It's She has a dog / She's got a dog
📝 When to Use HAVE:
Possession:
I have a laptop. / She has two sisters.
Physical characteristics:
He has blue eyes. / They have long hair.
Experiences:
We have dinner at 7 PM. / She has a meeting today.
Obligations (have to):
I have to study. / He has to work tomorrow.

💡 HAVE vs HAVE GOT

In British English, "have got" is very common for possession. Both are correct:

  • I have a car. = I have got a car. = I've got a car.
  • She has three children. = She has got three children.

❓ HAVE - Negative and Questions

Negative Forms:

With DO/DOES (American English):
  • I don't have a car.
  • She doesn't have time.
  • They don't have money.
With HAVE GOT (British English):
  • I haven't got a car.
  • She hasn't got time.
  • They haven't got money.

Question Forms:

With DO/DOES:
  • Do you have a pen? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  • Does he have a car? → Yes, he does. / No, he doesn't.
With HAVE GOT:
  • Have you got a pen? → Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
  • Has he got a car? → Yes, he has. / No, he hasn't.

🏠 THERE IS / THERE ARE - Existence and Location

THERE IS / THERE ARE is one of the most important structures in English. It is used to say that something exists or is located somewhere. This structure is different from using BE alone because it introduces new information.

Basic Rules:

Structure When to Use Examples
THERE IS Singular nouns There is a book on the table.
THERE IS Uncountable nouns There is water in the bottle.
THERE ARE Plural nouns There are three students here.

💡 Important Rule

When you have a list and the first item is singular, use THERE IS:

  • There is a dog and two cats in the yard.
  • There are two cats and a dog in the yard.
📝 Common Uses:
Describing a place:
There is a bank on this street. / There are many shops downtown.
Talking about quantity:
There are 30 students in my class. / There is only one chair.
Events:
There is a party tonight. / There are concerts every weekend.

🔍 THERE IS/ARE - Negative and Questions

Negative Forms:

Full Form Contraction Example
There is not There isn't / There's not There isn't any milk.
There are not There aren't There aren't any chairs.
📝 Negative Examples:
There isn't a solution to this problem.
There aren't enough seats for everyone.
There's no coffee left. (informal)

Question Forms:

Yes/No Questions - Invert THERE and IS/ARE:
  • Is there a bathroom here? → Yes, there is. / No, there isn't.
  • Are there any questions? → Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.
  • Is there a post office nearby? → Yes, there is one on Main Street.
HOW MANY/MUCH Questions:
  • How many students are there? → There are 25.
  • How much time is there? → There is one hour.
  • How many books are there on the shelf? → There are ten.

⚖️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Confusing IT IS with THERE IS

❌ Wrong:

It is a book on the table.

✅ Correct:

There is a book on the table.

Use THERE IS to introduce something new. Use IT IS to refer to something already mentioned or known.

Mistake 2: Plural Agreement

❌ Wrong:

There is three books.

✅ Correct:

There are three books.

Mistake 3: Using HAVE for Age

❌ Wrong:

I have 25 years old.

✅ Correct:

I am 25 years old.

Use BE for age, not HAVE!

Mistake 4: Double Subject with THERE IS

❌ Wrong:

There is it a problem.

✅ Correct:

There is a problem.

🌍 THERE IS/ARE in Different Tenses

THERE IS/ARE can be used in different tenses by changing the form of BE.

Tense Singular Plural Example
Present Simple There is There are There is a meeting today.
Past Simple There was There were There was a party yesterday.
Future There will be There will be There will be a concert tomorrow.
Present Perfect There has been There have been There has been a problem.
📝 Examples in Different Tenses:
Past:
There was a beautiful sunset last night.
Past:
There were many people at the beach.
Future:
There will be a test next week.
Present Perfect:
There have been many changes recently.

📊 THERE IS/ARE with Quantifiers

THERE IS/ARE is commonly used with words that express quantity.

With Positive Quantities:
  • There are some cookies in the jar.
  • There are many students in the library.
  • There are a lot of cars on the highway.
  • There are several options available.
  • There are a few mistakes in your essay.
  • There is much work to do. (formal)
  • There is a little sugar left.
With Negative Quantities:
  • There aren't any books on the shelf.
  • There is no time to waste.
  • There are no students in the classroom.
  • There isn't much coffee left.
  • There aren't many people here.

💡 ANY vs SOME

  • Use SOME in positive sentences: There are some apples.
  • Use ANY in negative sentences: There aren't any apples.
  • Use ANY in questions: Are there any apples?
  • Use SOME in offers/requests: Would you like some coffee?

🏢 THERE IS/ARE vs IT IS - Key Differences

Many students confuse THERE IS/ARE with IT IS. Understanding the difference is crucial for natural English.

When to Use THERE IS/ARE:

1. Introducing something new:
  • There is a new restaurant downtown. (First mention)
  • There are some interesting books here. (New information)
2. Talking about existence or presence:
  • There is life on Earth.
  • There are 365 days in a year.
3. Describing what's in a place:
  • There is a park near my house.
  • There are two bathrooms in the apartment.

When to Use IT IS:

1. Referring to something already mentioned:
  • There is a restaurant downtown. It is very expensive. (Already mentioned)
2. Talking about time, weather, distance:
  • It is 3 o'clock.
  • It is raining.
  • It is 5 kilometers to the beach.
3. Giving opinions or making general statements:
  • It is important to study.
  • It is difficult to learn Chinese.
  • It is nice to meet you.
📝 Compare:
There is a book on the table. (Introducing/locating)
It is an interesting book. (Describing known item)
There is a problem. (Stating existence)
It is a serious problem. (Describing known problem)

🎓 Advanced Uses of BE and HAVE

BE in Continuous Tenses (as auxiliary verb):
  • I am studying English. (Present Continuous)
  • She was working yesterday. (Past Continuous)
  • They will be arriving soon. (Future Continuous)
  • We have been waiting for an hour. (Present Perfect Continuous)
BE in Passive Voice:
  • The book is written in English.
  • The house was built in 1990.
  • English is spoken all over the world.
  • The project will be completed next month.
HAVE as auxiliary verb (Perfect Tenses):
  • I have finished my homework. (Present Perfect)
  • She has lived here for 5 years. (Present Perfect)
  • They had left before I arrived. (Past Perfect)
  • We will have completed the project by Friday. (Future Perfect)
HAVE TO - Expressing Obligation:
  • I have to go now. (obligation)
  • She has to work on weekends. (obligation)
  • Do you have to wear a uniform? (question)
  • You don't have to come if you're busy. (no obligation)

✨ Useful Expressions with BE and HAVE

Common BE Expressions:
  • be afraid - I am afraid of spiders.
  • be angry - He is angry at me.
  • be careful - Be careful!
  • be hungry/thirsty - I'm hungry.
  • be late/early - Don't be late!
  • be ready - Are you ready?
  • be right/wrong - You are right.
  • be sure - I'm sure about it.
Common HAVE Expressions:
  • have breakfast/lunch/dinner
  • have a good time - We had a good time!
  • have fun - Have fun at the party!
  • have a look - Can I have a look?
  • have a shower/bath
  • have a meeting
  • have a problem
  • have a question
