🔍 Understanding HAVE: With GOT vs With DO

The verb HAVE can be used in two main ways in English: with DO/DOES (common in American English) and with HAVE GOT (common in British English). Both forms are correct and express possession, relationships, and characteristics.

💡 Key Differences

  • DO/DOES is used for questions and negatives in American English.
  • HAVE GOT is used for questions and negatives in British English.
  • Both forms mean the same thing and are interchangeable in meaning.

✅ Affirmative Forms:

Subject DO/DOES Form HAVE GOT Form Example
I/You/We/They have have got I have a bike / I've got a bike
He/She/It has has got She has a dog / She's got a dog

🚫 Negative Forms:

With DO/DOES (American English):
  • I don't have a car.
  • He doesn't have a phone.
  • We don't have time.
With HAVE GOT (British English):
  • I haven't got a car.
  • He hasn't got a phone.
  • We haven't got time.

❓ Question Forms:

With DO/DOES:
  • Do you have a pen? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  • Does she have a car? → Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
With HAVE GOT:
  • Have you got a pen? → Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
  • Has she got a car? → Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.
