⏱️ Perfect Tenses – Present Perfect or Present Perfect Progressive?

Both the Present Perfect and the Present Perfect Progressive describe actions that started in the past and continue into the present or have relevance now. But they focus on different aspects:

✅ Present Perfect

Used to emphasize the result or completion of an action.

Structure: Subject + has/have + past participle

Example:
I have read five chapters today.

🔄 Present Perfect Progressive

Used to emphasize the duration or ongoing nature of an action.

Structure: Subject + has/have + been + verb-ing

Example:
I have been reading for two hours.
More Examples:
Present Perfect:
She has written three emails this morning.
Present Perfect Progressive:
She has been writing emails since 8 a.m.
