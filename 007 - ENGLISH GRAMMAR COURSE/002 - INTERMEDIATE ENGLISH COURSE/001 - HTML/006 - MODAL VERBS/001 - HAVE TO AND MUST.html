🧭 Modal Verbs – Must and Have to

Must and have to are used to express obligation or necessity. They are often interchangeable, but there are subtle differences in usage and tone.

✅ Must

Used for strong personal obligation, rules, or recommendations. It sounds more formal or urgent.

Structure: Subject + must + base verb

Example:
You must wear a helmet when riding a bike.

✅ Have to

Used for external obligation (laws, rules, schedules). It’s more common in spoken English.

Structure: Subject + have/has to + base verb

Example:
I have to pay my taxes by April.
Comparison:
Must:
Students must arrive on time. (rule)
Have to:
I have to take my medicine every day. (necessity)
