🚫 Modal Verbs – Must Not, Don’t Have To, Don’t Need To, Needn’t

These modal verbs help us express prohibition and lack of necessity. They may look similar, but they have different meanings:

❌ Must Not

Used to express prohibition. It means something is not allowed.

Structure: Subject + must not + base verb

Example:
You must not smoke here. (It’s forbidden)

⭕ Don’t Have To / Don’t Need To / Needn’t

Used to express lack of obligation. It means something is not necessary, but it’s allowed.

Structure:

  • Subject + don’t/doesn’t have to + base verb
  • Subject + don’t/doesn’t need to + base verb
  • Subject + needn’t + base verb
Examples:
You don’t have to come early. (It’s optional)
She doesn’t need to bring anything. (It’s not required)
We needn’t worry about the weather. (It’s not necessary)
Comparison:
Must Not:
You must not touch the wires. (Dangerous!)
Don’t Have To:
You don’t have to stay late. (It’s your choice)
