📘 Infinitives and -ing Forms – “to” for Whole Infinitive

In English, the infinitive form of a verb is usually made with to + base verb. The word “to” is considered part of the whole infinitive. Some verbs, adjectives, and expressions are followed by the full infinitive, while others are not.

✅ Whole Infinitive Structure

to + base verb (e.g., to go, to eat, to study)

Examples: I want to learn. / She needs to rest.

✅ Common Uses of the Whole Infinitive

  • After certain verbs: want, need, decide, hope, plan
  • After adjectives: happy, ready, afraid, difficult
  • After question words: what, how, where, when
  • To express purpose: I study to improve my skills.
Examples:
Verb + infinitive:
They decided to travel in July.
Adjective + infinitive:
She is happy to help.
Question word + infinitive:
I don’t know what to say.
Purpose:
He studies hard to pass the exam.
