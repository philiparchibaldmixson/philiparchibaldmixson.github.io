🔎 Various Verb Structures – IT: Preparatory Subject

We use it as a preparatory subject when the real subject of the sentence is a clause or phrase that comes later. This structure helps us start sentences more naturally and clearly.

✅ Structure

It + be + adjective/noun + to-infinitive / that-clause

Examples: It is important to study. / It was surprising that she left early.

✅ Common Uses

  • With adjectives: It’s easy to understand. / It’s hard to believe.
  • With nouns: It’s a pleasure to meet you.
  • With that-clauses: It’s clear that he knows the answer.
  • To express opinions, feelings, or facts.
Examples:
Adjective + infinitive:
It is difficult to explain this rule.
Noun + infinitive:
It’s a good idea to rest before the test.
That-clause:
It’s obvious that she worked hard.
Opinion:
It’s important to be honest.
