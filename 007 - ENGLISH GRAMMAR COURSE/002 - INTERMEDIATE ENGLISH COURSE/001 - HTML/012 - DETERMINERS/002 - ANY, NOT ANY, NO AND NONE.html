🚫 Determiners – Any, Not Any, No, None

We use any, not any, no, and none to talk about absence, quantity, or choices. These determiners help us express negative ideas or lack of something.

✅ Usage Summary

  • Any – used in questions and negatives: Do you have any sugar? / I don’t have any money.
  • Not any – emphasizes complete absence: There aren’t any apples left.
  • No – used before a noun to show zero quantity: There is no milk in the fridge.
  • None – pronoun used without a noun: None of the answers were correct.

✅ Notes

  • No is more direct and emphatic than not any.
  • None can be used with “of”: None of them, None of the books.
  • Use any with plural or uncountable nouns.
Examples:
Any (question):
Do you have any questions?
Not any (negative):
There aren’t any cookies left.
No (emphatic):
There is no time to waste.
None (pronoun):
None of the students arrived on time.
