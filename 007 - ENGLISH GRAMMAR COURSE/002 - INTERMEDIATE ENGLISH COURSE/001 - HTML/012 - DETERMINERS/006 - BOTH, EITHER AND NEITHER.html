🧭 Determiners – Both, Either, and Neither

Both, either, and neither are used to refer to two people or things. They help us express inclusion, choice, or exclusion.

✅ Usage Summary

  • Both – refers to two things together: Both options are good.
  • Either – refers to one of two options (not both): You can choose either book.
  • Neither – refers to not one and not the other: Neither answer is correct.

✅ Notes

  • Used with singular nouns: either/neither + singular noun
  • Used with plural nouns: both + plural noun
  • Can be used with “of”: both of / either of / neither of + determiner (the/my/these)
Examples:
Both:
Both teams played well.
Either:
You can take either road.
Neither:
Neither option is suitable.
With “of”:
Both of the books are interesting.
