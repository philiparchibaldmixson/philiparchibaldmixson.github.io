👥 Nouns – Mixed Singular and Plural

Some collective nouns can be treated as either singular or plural depending on the context and the speaker’s intention. This is especially common in British English.

✅ Collective Nouns

  • Refer to a group of people or things: family, team, staff, government, class
  • Can take a singular or plural verb depending on whether the group is seen as a unit or as individuals

✅ Examples

  • Singular: My family is large. (the family as one unit)
  • Plural: My family are angry with me. (individual members are angry)

✅ Notes

  • In American English, collective nouns are usually singular: The team is winning.
  • In British English, plural usage is more common: The team are wearing their new uniforms.
Examples:
Plural meaning:
My family are angry with me.
Singular meaning:
The team is playing well today.
Plural meaning:
The staff are preparing for the event.
Singular meaning:
The government is introducing new policies.

Exercise 1: Choose singular or plural
  • The class __________ (is / are) taking a test today.
  • My family __________ (is / are) planning a vacation.
  • The team __________ (is / are) arguing among themselves.
  • The staff __________ (is / are) very friendly.
  • The audience __________ (was / were) clapping loudly.
Exercise 2: Rewrite the sentence
  • My team is excited. (plural meaning)
  • The government are divided. (singular meaning)
  • The family is supportive. (plural meaning)
  • The staff is helpful. (plural meaning)
  • The class are noisy. (singular meaning)
