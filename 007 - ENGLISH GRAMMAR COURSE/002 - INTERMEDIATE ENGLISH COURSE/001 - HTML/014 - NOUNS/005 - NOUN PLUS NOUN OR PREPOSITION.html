🔗 Nouns – Noun + Noun or Preposition

We often combine nouns to describe things. Sometimes we use noun + noun (called compound nouns), and sometimes we use noun + preposition + noun to show relationships or details.

✅ Noun + Noun

  • The first noun acts like an adjective: chicken soup, car door, school bus
  • Usually no article between the nouns
  • Common in short, everyday phrases

✅ Noun + Preposition + Noun

  • Used to show more complex relationships: the door of the car, the name of the street
  • Often used in formal or written English
  • Includes prepositions like of, in, on, for, with

✅ Comparison

  • School bus = a type of bus
  • The bus of the school = the bus that belongs to the school
Examples:
Noun + Noun:
We took the mountain road.
Noun + Preposition + Noun:
We drove along the road through the mountains.
Noun + Noun:
She opened the car door.
Noun + Preposition + Noun:
She looked at the door of the car.
