🧠 Adjectives and Adverbs – Adjectives Without Nouns

Adjectives usually describe nouns, but sometimes they are used without a noun. In these cases, the noun is understood from context or is general. This is common in expressions like “the blind,” “the rich,” “the poor”.

✅ Structure

  • the + adjective → refers to a group of people
  • Examples: the blind, the elderly, the unemployed
  • These expressions are plural and take plural verbs

✅ Meaning

  • the blind = people who are blind
  • the rich = people who are rich
  • the sick = people who are sick

✅ Example Phrase

  • In the country of the blind = among people who are blind
  • This phrase is often used metaphorically to mean: “Among people who lack knowledge, someone with a little knowledge seems powerful.”
Examples:
Group of people:
We must help the poor.
Metaphorical:
In the country of the blind, the one-eyed man is king.
General reference:
The elderly need special care.
Plural verb:
The unemployed are looking for jobs.
