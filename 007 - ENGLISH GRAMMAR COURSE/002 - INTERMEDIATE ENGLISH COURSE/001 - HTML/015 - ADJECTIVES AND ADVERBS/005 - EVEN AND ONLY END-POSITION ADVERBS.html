✨ Adjectives and Adverbs – Even and Only; End-Position Adverbs

Even and only are adverbs that add emphasis or limitation. Their position in the sentence affects the meaning. End-position adverbs are placed at the end of a clause and usually describe time, manner, or place.

✅ Even

  • Used to show surprise or emphasis
  • Example: She even remembered my birthday.
  • Can appear before the verb or noun it emphasizes

✅ Only

  • Used to limit or restrict meaning
  • Example: He only eats fruit.
  • Placement changes the meaning: Only he eats fruit vs. He eats only fruit

✅ End-Position Adverbs

  • Usually describe time, manner, or place
  • Examples: today, quickly, at home
  • Placed at the end of the sentence or clause
Examples:
Even:
She even brought dessert.
Only (subject):
Only John passed the test.
Only (object):
John passed only the test.
End-position adverb:
They arrived late.
End-position adverb:
She spoke quietly.

  • I saw him __________ (yesterday / yesterdayly).
  • She goes to the gym __________ (often / frequent).
