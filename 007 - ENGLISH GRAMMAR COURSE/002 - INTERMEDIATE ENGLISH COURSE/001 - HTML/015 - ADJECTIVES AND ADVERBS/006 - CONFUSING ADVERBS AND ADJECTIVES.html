⚡ Adjectives and Adverbs – Confusing Adjectives and Adverbs

Some words look the same as both adjectives and adverbs. Others have different forms. It’s important to know how they work with verbs and nouns.

✅ Same Form: Adjective = Adverb

  • Fast – He is a fast runner. / He runs fast.
  • Hard – This is a hard task. / She works hard.
  • Late – A late train. / He arrived late.

✅ Different Forms

  • Good (adj) vs. Well (adv) – She is a good singer. / She sings well.
  • Hard (adj) vs. Hardly (adv) – It’s a hard job. / He hardly sleeps. (≠ hard)
  • Late (adj) vs. Lately (adv) – A late guest. / I haven’t seen him lately.

✅ Notes

  • “Fast,” “hard,” and “late” are both adjectives and adverbs with the same form.
  • “Well,” “hardly,” and “lately” are adverbs with different meanings.
  • Be careful with “hardly” and “lately” – they do not mean “hard” or “late.”
Examples:
Fast:
He drives fast.
Hard:
She works hard every day.
Late:
They arrived late to the meeting.
Hardly:
He hardly spoke during dinner.
Lately:
I haven’t seen her lately.
