🧩 Conjunctions – Leaving Out “That”

In English, we often use the conjunction that to introduce a subordinate clause. However, in informal or spoken English, we can leave out “that” when the meaning is clear.

✅ When Can We Leave Out “That”?

  • After common reporting verbs: say, know, think, believe, hope
  • When the clause is the object of the verb
  • Example: She knew (that) I was right.

✅ When Should We Keep “That”?

  • To avoid confusion or ambiguity
  • In formal writing or complex sentences
  • Example: I heard that she left early.

✅ Common Verbs Where “That” Can Be Dropped

  • know, think, say, believe, hear, hope, feel, understand
Examples:
With “that”:
She said that she was tired.
Without “that”:
She said she was tired.
With “that”:
He knew that I was right.
Without “that”:
He knew I was right.
Formal:
We believe that the results are accurate.
