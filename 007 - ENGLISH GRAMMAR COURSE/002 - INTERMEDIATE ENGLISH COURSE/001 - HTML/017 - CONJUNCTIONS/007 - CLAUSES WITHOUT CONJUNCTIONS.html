🧩 Conjunctions – Clauses Without Conjunctions

Sometimes we can express actions or conditions without using a conjunction like after, because, or when. Instead, we use a participle phrase (often starting with an -ing or -ed verb) to replace the full clause. This makes the sentence shorter and more fluent.

✅ Structure

  • -ing form → active meaning: Putting down my book, I stood up.
  • -ed form → passive or completed meaning: Exhausted, she went to bed.
  • These phrases often replace time or reason clauses
  • They are used when the subject of both actions is the same

✅ Common Uses

  • Time: Finishing his meal, he left.
  • Cause: Feeling tired, she stayed home.
  • Condition: Given enough time, we’ll succeed.
Examples:
Time:
Putting down my book, I turned off the light.
Cause:
Feeling nervous, he didn’t speak.
Condition:
Given enough support, she’ll succeed.
Passive:
Surprised by the news, they paused in silence.
