🔗 Relatives – What = The Thing(s) That

What can be used to mean “the thing(s) that”. It introduces a noun clause and acts as both the subject and object of the clause. This structure is common in spoken and written English.

✅ Structure

  • What + subject + verb = “the thing(s) that…”
  • Example: Take what you like. = Take the things that you like.
  • “What” replaces “the thing(s) that” and makes the sentence more natural

✅ Usage

  • Used to refer to something unknown or general
  • Often used in commands, statements, or indirect questions
  • Examples: What you said was true. / I didn’t hear what she said.
Examples:
Command:
Take what you like. (Take the things that you like)
Statement:
What he said surprised me. (The thing that he said)
Indirect question:
I don’t know what she wants. (The thing that she wants)
General reference:
He gave me what I needed. (The thing that I needed)
