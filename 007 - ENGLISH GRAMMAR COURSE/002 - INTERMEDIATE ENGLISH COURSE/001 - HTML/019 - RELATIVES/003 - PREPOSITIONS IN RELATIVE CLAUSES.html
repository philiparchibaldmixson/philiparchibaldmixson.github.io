🔗 Relatives – Prepositions in Relative Clauses

In relative clauses, prepositions can be placed before the relative pronoun (formal) or at the end of the clause (informal). The choice depends on the level of formality and the structure of the sentence.

✅ Informal Structure

  • ...which/that + subject + verb + preposition
  • Example: This is the chair that I sat on.
  • Preposition comes at the end of the clause

✅ Formal Structure

  • Preposition + which/whom
  • Example: This is the chair on which I sat.
  • Used in more formal writing or speech

✅ Notes

  • We use which for things and whom for people in formal style
  • We do not use that after a preposition
  • In informal English, who or which is often omitted
Examples:
Informal:
This is the girl who I spoke to.
Formal:
This is the girl to whom I spoke.
Informal:
That’s the house which I lived in.
Formal:
That’s the house in which I lived.
