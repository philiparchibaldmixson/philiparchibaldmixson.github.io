✂️ Relatives – Reduced Relative Clauses

A reduced relative clause is a shorter form of a full relative clause. It removes the relative pronoun (who, which, that) and auxiliary verb, and often uses a participle (verb ending in -ing or -ed). This makes sentences more concise and formal.

✅ Passive Reduction (Past Participle)

  • Full clause: Luggage that was left unattended will be removed.
  • Reduced clause: Luggage left unattended will be removed.
  • Use the past participle when the subject receives the action

✅ Active Reduction (Present Participle)

  • Full clause: Passengers who are waiting in line should stay calm.
  • Reduced clause: Passengers waiting in line should stay calm.
  • Use the present participle when the subject does the action

✅ Notes

  • Only possible when the subject of both clauses is the same
  • Common in formal writing, signs, and instructions
  • Can be used with people, things, or places
Examples:
Passive:
Luggage left unattended will be removed. (Luggage that was left unattended)
Active:
People waiting outside should come in. (People who are waiting outside)
Passive:
Books written by her are bestsellers. (Books that were written by her)
Active:
Students preparing for exams need quiet. (Students who are preparing)
