📘 Relatives – Non-identifying Relative Clauses

A non-identifying relative clause gives extra information about a noun that is already clear or specific. It is not essential to the meaning of the sentence and is separated by commas. These clauses help add detail or context.

✅ Structure

  • Noun, relative pronoun + clause
  • Example: Kelly, who does my hair, is on vacation.
  • Use commas before and after the clause

✅ Usage

  • Used to add extra, non-essential information
  • Common relative pronouns: who, which, whose
  • Do not use “that” in non-identifying clauses
Examples:
Person:
Kelly, who does my hair, is very talented.
Thing:
The laptop, which I bought last year, is still working well.
Possession:
My neighbor, whose garden is beautiful, won an award.
Group:
The musicians, who performed last night, received a standing ovation.
