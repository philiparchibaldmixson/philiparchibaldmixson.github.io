📖 Relatives – Reading Sentences with Relative Clauses

Relative clauses give more information about a noun. When reading sentences with relative clauses, it’s important to identify the main clause and the extra information added by the relative clause. This helps improve comprehension and fluency.

✅ How to Read Relative Clauses

  • Look for relative pronouns: who, which, that, whose, where, when
  • Pause slightly before and after the clause (especially with commas)
  • Understand what noun the clause is describing
  • Decide if the clause is essential (identifying) or extra (non-identifying)

✅ Examples of Reading with Meaning

  • Kelly, who does my hair, is on vacation. → Extra detail about Kelly
  • The book that I borrowed was excellent. → Identifies which book
  • The man whose car was stolen reported it to the police. → Possession
Examples:
Non-identifying:
Kelly, who does my hair, is very friendly.
Identifying:
The student who won the prize is my cousin.
Possession:
The artist whose work was displayed is famous.
Place:
This is the café where we met last week.
