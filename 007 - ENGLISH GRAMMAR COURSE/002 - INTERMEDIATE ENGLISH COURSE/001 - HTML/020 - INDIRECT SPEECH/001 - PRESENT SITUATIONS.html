🗣️ Indirect Speech – Present Situations

In indirect speech, we report what someone said without quoting their exact words. When the original statement is about a general truth or a present fact, we can keep the present tense even if the reporting verb is in the past.

✅ Structure

  • Reporting verb (past) + that + present/past verb
  • Example: He proved that the Earth is round.
  • We can also say: He proved that the Earth was round. (less common, more formal)

✅ Usage

  • Use present tense for facts and general truths: She said that water boils at 100°C.
  • Use past tense for completed actions or when distancing the statement
  • Both “is” and “was” may be correct depending on context
Examples:
General truth:
He proved that the Earth is round.
Formal/past style:
He proved that the Earth was round.
Scientific fact:
She explained that light travels faster than sound.
Neutral reporting:
They confirmed that the results are accurate.
