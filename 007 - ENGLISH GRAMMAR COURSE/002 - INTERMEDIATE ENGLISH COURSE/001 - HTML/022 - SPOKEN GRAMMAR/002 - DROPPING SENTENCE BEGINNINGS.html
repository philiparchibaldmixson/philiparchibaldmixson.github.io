🗣️ Spoken Grammar – Dropping Sentence Beginnings

In spoken English, we often drop the beginning of a sentence, especially when the meaning is clear from context. This makes speech faster and more natural. For example, instead of saying “I must dash,” we simply say “Must dash.”

✅ Structure

  • Verb phrase without subject
  • Example: Must dash. (instead of “I must dash.”)
  • Used in informal speech, especially when the subject is obvious

✅ Usage

  • Common in everyday conversation
  • Often used with modal verbs: must, should, could, might
  • Helps express urgency, emotion, or routine
Examples:
Urgency:
Must dash, I’m late!
Suggestion:
Should go, really.
Possibility:
Might see her later.
Routine:
Have to work tomorrow.
