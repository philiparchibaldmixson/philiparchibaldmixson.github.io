🗣️ Spoken Grammar – Dropping Words After Auxiliaries

In spoken English, we often drop words after auxiliary verbs when the meaning is clear from context. This makes speech faster and more natural. For example, instead of saying “Get up now!” we simply say “Get up!”, and instead of “I am ready,” we say “I am!”

✅ Structure

  • Auxiliary/modal verb + (optional complement)
  • Examples: Get up!, I am!, She can!
  • Used when the rest of the sentence is understood or implied

✅ Usage

  • Common in everyday conversation
  • Often used with modal verbs (can, should, will) and auxiliaries (be, have)
  • Helps express agreement, urgency, or contrast
Examples:
Command:
Get up! You’re going to be late.
Agreement:
“Are you ready?” – I am!
Contrast:
“She can’t swim.” – She can! I saw her.
Confirmation:
“Will you help?” – I will.
