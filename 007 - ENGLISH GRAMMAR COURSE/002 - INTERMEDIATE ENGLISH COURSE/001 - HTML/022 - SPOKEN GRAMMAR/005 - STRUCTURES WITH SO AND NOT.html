🗣️ Spoken Grammar – Structures with So and Not

In spoken English, we often use short expressions like I think so, I don’t think so, I hope so, and I hope not to respond quickly and naturally. These structures avoid repeating the full sentence when the meaning is clear.

✅ Structure

  • Verb + so/not
  • Examples: I think so, I don’t think so, I hope so, I hope not
  • “So” replaces a positive idea; “not” replaces a negative one

✅ Usage

  • Used to agree, disagree, or express wishes
  • Common in everyday conversation
  • Helps avoid repetition
Examples:
Agreement:
“Will it rain?” – I think so.
Disagreement:
“Is he coming?” – I don’t think so.
Positive wish:
“Will we win?” – I hope so.
Negative wish:
“Will it be canceled?” – I hope not.
