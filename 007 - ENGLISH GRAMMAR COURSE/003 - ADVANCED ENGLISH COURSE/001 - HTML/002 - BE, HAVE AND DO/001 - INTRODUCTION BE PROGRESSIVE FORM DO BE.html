Be, Have and Do

Advanced Grammar – Introduction and BE Forms

Introduction to Be, Have and Do

The verbs be, have, and do are auxiliary verbs that also function as main verbs. They are essential for forming questions, negatives, and progressive or perfect tenses.

Key Point: These verbs are highly irregular and behave differently depending on their role in the sentence.

BE: Progressive Forms

The verb be is used to form progressive (continuous) tenses. It is followed by the present participle (-ing form) of the main verb.

Structure

Subject + be (am/is/are/was/were) + verb-ing

She is studying for her exams.
They were watching a movie.
I am working late tonight.
We are learning advanced grammar.
Tip: The form of “be” changes with tense and subject, but the main verb always ends in -ing.

DO BE – Emphatic and Elliptical Use

In spoken and informal English, do be is sometimes used for emphasis or in elliptical structures. It’s not standard in formal writing but appears in expressions like:

Do be careful!
He does be working hard lately. (non-standard)
They do be noisy sometimes. (dialectal)
Note: “Do be” is often used in regional dialects or for emphasis. In standard grammar, “do” is not normally used with “be” as a main verb.
