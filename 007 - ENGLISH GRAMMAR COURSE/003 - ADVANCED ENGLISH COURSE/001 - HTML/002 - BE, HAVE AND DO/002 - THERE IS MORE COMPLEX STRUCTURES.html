Advanced Grammar – There Is: More Complex Structures

Understanding “There Is” and “There Are”

The structure “there is” or “there are” is used to say that something exists or is present. These forms are common in both spoken and written English and can be expanded into more complex structures.

Key Point: “There is/are” introduces new information and is often followed by a noun phrase.

More Complex Forms of “There Is”

1. With Modals

We can use modal verbs with “there is” to express possibility, necessity, or permission.

There must be a mistake.
There could be a better way.
There should be more options.

2. With Perfect Tenses

We can use “there has/have been” to talk about past events or experiences.

There has been an accident.
There have been many changes.

3. With Progressive Forms

Use “there is/are + being + past participle” to describe ongoing actions in passive voice.

There is being a lot said about the topic. (rare)
There are being improvements made. (formal)

4. With Infinitives

Use “there is/are + to + verb” to express future plans or expectations.

There is to be a meeting tomorrow.
There are to be changes in the policy.
Note: These advanced forms are more common in formal writing and official announcements.
