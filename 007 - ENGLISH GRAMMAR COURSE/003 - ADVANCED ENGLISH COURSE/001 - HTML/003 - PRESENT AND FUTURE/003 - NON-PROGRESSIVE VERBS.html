Advanced Grammar – Non-Progressive Verbs

What Are Non-Progressive Verbs?

Non-progressive (or stative) verbs describe states, feelings, thoughts, and senses rather than actions. These verbs are usually not used in continuous (progressive) forms, even when talking about the present or future.

Key Point: Non-progressive verbs are typically used in the simple or perfect tenses, not in -ing forms.

Common Non-Progressive Verbs

Verbs of Emotion

I love this song. (Not: I am loving this song.)
She hates waiting.

Verbs of Possession

He has a new phone. (Not: He is having a new phone.)
We own a small business.

Verbs of Thought and Belief

I believe you. (Not: I am believing you.)
She knows the answer.

Verbs of Perception

This cake smells delicious.
It sounds like a good idea.
Note: Some verbs can be used in progressive forms when they describe actions, not states. Example: “He is having lunch” (action) vs. “He has a car” (state).
