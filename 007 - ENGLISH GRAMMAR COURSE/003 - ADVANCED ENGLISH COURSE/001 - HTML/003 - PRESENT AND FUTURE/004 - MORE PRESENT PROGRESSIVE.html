Present and Future

Advanced Grammar – More About the Present Progressive: Going to and Will

Present Progressive for Future Plans

The present progressive (am/is/are + verb-ing) is often used to talk about future arrangements or plans that are already decided.

I’m meeting Anna at 5 p.m.
We’re flying to Paris next week.
She’s starting her new job tomorrow.
Tip: Use this form when the plan is definite and scheduled.

Going to for Intentions and Predictions

Going to is used to express future intentions or predictions based on current evidence.

Intentions

I’m going to learn Spanish this year.
They’re going to start a business.

Predictions

Look at those clouds! It’s going to rain.
She’s going to win the competition.

Will for Decisions and Promises

Will is used for spontaneous decisions, promises, offers, and predictions not based on current evidence.

Decisions and Promises

I’ll help you with your homework.
We’ll call you later.

General Predictions

People will travel to Mars someday.
It’ll be a great party.
Compare: “I’m going to visit grandma” (intention) vs. “I’ll visit grandma” (decision made now).
