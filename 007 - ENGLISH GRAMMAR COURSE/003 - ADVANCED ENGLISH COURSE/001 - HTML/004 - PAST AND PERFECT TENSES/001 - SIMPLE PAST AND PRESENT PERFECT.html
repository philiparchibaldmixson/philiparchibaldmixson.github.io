Advanced Grammar – Introduction, Simple Past & Present Perfect Summary

Introduction to Past and Perfect Tenses

Past and perfect tenses help us describe actions that happened before now. The simple past focuses on completed actions at a specific time, while the present perfect connects past actions to the present moment.

Key Point: Use the simple past for finished events and the present perfect for experiences or results that still matter now.

Simple Past

Used for actions that happened at a specific time in the past. Time expressions like “yesterday,” “last week,” or “in 2010” are common.

I visited Rome last summer.
She graduated in 2020.
They watched a movie yesterday.

Present Perfect

Used for actions that happened at an unspecified time or have relevance now. Common time expressions include “already,” “yet,” “ever,” and “just.”

I’ve visited Rome twice.
She has already finished her homework.
Have you ever been to Japan?
