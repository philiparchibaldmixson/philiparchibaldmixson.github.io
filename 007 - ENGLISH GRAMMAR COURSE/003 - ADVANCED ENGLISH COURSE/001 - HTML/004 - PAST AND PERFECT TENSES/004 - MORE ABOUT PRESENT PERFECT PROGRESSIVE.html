Advanced Grammar – More About Present Perfect Progressive

Understanding the Present Perfect Progressive

The present perfect progressive (also called present perfect continuous) describes actions that started in the past and are still continuing or have recently stopped. It emphasizes the duration or ongoing nature of the activity.

Structure: have/has + been + verb-ing

Common Uses of Present Perfect Progressive

Actions Continuing Until Now

I’ve been studying all morning.
She has been working here since 2019.

Recently Completed Actions

We’ve been cleaning the house—it’s finally done.
He’s been exercising, so he’s tired now.

Emphasizing Duration

They’ve been waiting for over an hour.
I’ve been thinking about that idea all week.
