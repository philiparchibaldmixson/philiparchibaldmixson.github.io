Modal Verbs

Advanced Grammar – Introduction and Ability: Can and Could

Introduction to Modal Verbs

Modal verbs are auxiliary verbs that express ability, possibility, permission, or obligation. They are followed by the base form of the verb and do not change form for tense or subject.

Examples of modal verbs: can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would

Expressing Ability: Can and Could

Can – Present or General Ability

I can speak three languages.
She can swim very well.

Could – Past Ability or Polite Possibility

When I was younger, I could run fast.
Could you help me with this?

Negative Forms

I can’t play the piano.
He couldn’t find his keys.
Tip: Use “can” for present ability and “could” for past ability or polite requests.
