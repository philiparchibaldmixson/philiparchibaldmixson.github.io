Modal Verbs

Advanced Grammar – Obligation: Must and Have (Got) To

Modal Verbs for Obligation

Must and have to express strong obligation or necessity. They are often interchangeable, but “must” is more formal or personal, while “have to” is more common in everyday speech. Have got to is an informal variant of “have to.”

Structure: must + base verb / have to + base verb / have got to + base verb

Usage and Examples

Must – Strong Personal Obligation

You must wear a seatbelt.
I must finish this report today.

Have to – External or General Obligation

We have to pay taxes.
She has to attend the meeting.

Have Got to – Informal Spoken English

I’ve got to go now.
You’ve got to see this movie!

Negative Forms

You must not smoke here. (prohibition)
You don’t have to come if you’re busy. (lack of necessity)
Tip: “Must not” means something is forbidden. “Don’t have to” means it’s optional.
