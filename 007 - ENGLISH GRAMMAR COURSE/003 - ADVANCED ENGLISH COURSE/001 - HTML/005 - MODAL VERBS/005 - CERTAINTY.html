Advanced Grammar – Certainty: Must, Can't, Will, Should

Modal Verbs for Certainty

Modal verbs like must, can’t, will, and should express different degrees of certainty or logical deduction. They help us make conclusions based on evidence or expectations.

Structure: modal + base verb (e.g., “She must be tired.”)

Usage and Examples

Must – Strong Deduction or Certainty

He must be at home—his car is outside.
You must be joking!

Can't – Strong Negative Deduction

She can’t be serious.
That can’t be true.

Will – Future Certainty or Strong Prediction

He will succeed if he keeps working hard.
This will be the best day ever.

Should – Expected Outcome or Logical Guess

They should arrive soon—it’s almost 6 p.m.
The results should be ready by tomorrow.
Tip: “Must” and “can’t” show strong certainty. “Will” predicts confidently. “Should” suggests expectation.
