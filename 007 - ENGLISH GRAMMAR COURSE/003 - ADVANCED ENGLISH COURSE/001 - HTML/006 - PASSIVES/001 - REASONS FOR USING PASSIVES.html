Passives

Advanced Grammar – Introduction and Reasons for Using Passives

What Is the Passive Voice?

The passive voice is used when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action, rather than the doer. It is formed with the verb “to be” + past participle.

Structure: subject + be (in correct tense) + past participle (+ by agent, optional)

Reasons for Using the Passive

1. When the agent is unknown or unimportant

The documents were stolen.
The window was broken last night.

2. To emphasize the action or result

The project was completed ahead of schedule.
The cake was eaten before the party started.

3. To be more formal or impersonal

Guests are requested to remain seated.
The issue will be addressed by the committee.
Tip: Use the passive voice to shift focus, avoid blame, or sound more objective.
