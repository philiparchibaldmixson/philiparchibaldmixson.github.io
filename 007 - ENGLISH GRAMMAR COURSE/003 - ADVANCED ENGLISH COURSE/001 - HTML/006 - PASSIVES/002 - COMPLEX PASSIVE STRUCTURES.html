Advanced Grammar – Complex Passive Structures

What Are Complex Passive Structures?

Complex passive structures are used when the subject of the sentence is followed by a reporting verb (e.g., say, believe, think, know) and an infinitive. These are often used in formal or academic writing to report opinions or beliefs.

Structure 1: It + passive reporting verb + that-clause
Structure 2: Subject + passive reporting verb + to-infinitive

Examples of Complex Passives

Impersonal Passive (It + is said that...)

It is believed that the company will expand.
It was reported that she had resigned.

Personal Passive (Subject + is said to...)

The company is believed to be expanding.
She is reported to have resigned.

Passive with Modal Verbs

The documents must be signed by the manager.
The issue should have been resolved earlier.
Tip: Use complex passives to sound more objective, formal, or to avoid naming the agent.
