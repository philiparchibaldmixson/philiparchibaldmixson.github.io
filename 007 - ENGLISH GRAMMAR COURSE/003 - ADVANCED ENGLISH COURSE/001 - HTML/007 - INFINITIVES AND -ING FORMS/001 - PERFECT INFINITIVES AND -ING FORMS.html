Infinitives and -ing Forms

Advanced Grammar – Introduction and Perfect Forms

Introduction to Infinitives and -ing Forms

Infinitives (to + base verb) and -ing forms (verb + -ing) are used in many grammatical structures. They can act as subjects, objects, or complements. Choosing between them depends on the verb, meaning, and context.

Examples: I want to leave. / She enjoys reading.

Perfect Infinitives and Perfect -ing Forms

Perfect Infinitive: to have + past participle

Used to refer to a past action, especially after modal verbs or expressions of regret, expectation, or speculation.

She seems to have forgotten the meeting.
He was happy to have passed the exam.

Perfect -ing Form: having + past participle

Used to emphasize that one action happened before another, often in formal writing or complex sentences.

Having finished his work, he went home.
She regrets having said that.
Tip: Use perfect forms to show time relationships or reflect on past actions with nuance.
