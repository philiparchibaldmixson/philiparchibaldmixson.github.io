Infinitives and -ing Forms

Advanced Grammar – Verb + Object + Infinitive or -ing Form

Understanding Verb + Object + Infinitive/-ing

Some verbs are followed by an object and then either an infinitive (to + base verb) or an -ing form (verb + -ing). The choice depends on the verb and sometimes changes the meaning.

Structure: verb + object + to-infinitive / verb + object + -ing
Examples: She advised him to wait. / I caught her stealing.

Common Patterns and Examples

Verb + Object + Infinitive

They encouraged her to apply.
I want you to succeed.

Verb + Object + -ing

I caught him cheating.
We heard them arguing.

Verbs That Can Take Both (with or without change in meaning)

She saw him cross the street. (whole action)
She saw him crossing the street. (in progress)
Tip: Some verbs (e.g., see, hear, watch, feel) can be followed by either form, but the meaning may differ.
