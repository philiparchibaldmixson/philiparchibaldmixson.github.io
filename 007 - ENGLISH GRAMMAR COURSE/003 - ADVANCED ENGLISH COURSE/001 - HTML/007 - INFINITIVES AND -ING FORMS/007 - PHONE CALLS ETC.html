Infinitives and -ing Forms

Advanced Grammar – Noun + Infinitive (e.g., phone calls to make, nothing to eat)

Understanding Noun + Infinitive Structures

We often use the infinitive after a noun to describe purpose, function, or necessity. These structures are common in spoken and written English and help express what needs to be done or what is available.

Structure: noun + to + base verb
Examples: I have phone calls to make. / There’s nothing to eat.

Usage and Examples

Describing Tasks or Responsibilities

I have emails to send before noon.
There are reports to review.

Describing Availability or Lack

There’s nothing to eat in the fridge.
We had nowhere to go.

Describing Purpose or Function

He needs a book to read on the plane.
She brought something to drink.
Tip: These structures are useful for expressing what is needed, missing, or intended.
