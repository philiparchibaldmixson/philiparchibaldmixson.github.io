Infinitives and -ing Forms

Advanced Grammar – Infinitives with Their Own Subject: For ... To

What Is “For + Subject + Infinitive”?

We use the structure for + subject + to-infinitive when the infinitive has its own subject. This is common when expressing purpose, necessity, or possibility, especially in formal writing.

Structure: for + noun/pronoun + to + base verb
Examples: It’s important for you to be on time. / There’s no need for them to worry.

Usage and Examples

Expressing Importance or Necessity

It’s essential for students to understand the rules.
It’s necessary for him to attend the meeting.

Expressing Possibility or Purpose

There’s a chance for us to win.
This is a good opportunity for her to travel.

Negative and Question Forms

Is it common for people to work remotely?
It’s not unusual for children to ask questions.
Tip: Use “for + subject + to” when the person doing the action is different from the main subject of the sentence.
