Verb Structures

Advanced Grammar – Object + Adjective or Noun Complement

Introduction to Verb Structures

Some verbs are followed by an object and then a complement that describes or identifies the object. These complements can be adjectives or nouns and are essential for expressing judgments, roles, or states.

Structure: verb + object + adjective/noun
Examples: They elected him president. / She found the task difficult.

Common Verbs with Object + Complement

Verbs with Object + Adjective

She considered the idea brilliant.
We found the room empty.

Verbs with Object + Noun

They named the baby Oliver.
The board appointed her manager.

Verbs of Perception and Judgment

I thought the movie a masterpiece.
He judged the plan a failure.
Tip: These structures are useful for expressing opinions, decisions, and roles clearly and concisely.
