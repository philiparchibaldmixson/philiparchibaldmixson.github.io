Verb Structures

Advanced Grammar – Causative Structures with Have, Get, Make

What Are Causative Structures?

Causative verbs are used when one person causes another person to do something. The most common causative verbs are have, get, and make. Each has a slightly different meaning and structure.

Have: subject + have + person + base verb
Get: subject + get + person + to + base verb
Make: subject + make + person + base verb

Usage and Examples

Using “Have”

She had the technician fix the computer.
I had my assistant call the client.

Using “Get”

We got him to join the team.
She got her brother to help with the move.

Using “Make”

The teacher made the students stay late.
They made me clean the room.
Tip: “Make” implies force, “Get” implies persuasion, and “Have” implies arrangement or instruction.
