Nouns and Pronouns

Advanced Grammar – Introduction and Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

Introduction to Nouns and Pronouns

Nouns name people, places, things, or ideas. Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition. Understanding how nouns behave—especially in terms of countability—is essential for mastering article use, quantifiers, and verb agreement.

Examples: Maria is a teacher. → She teaches English. / I need information.

Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

Countable Nouns

These nouns refer to things that can be counted. They have singular and plural forms and can be used with numbers and articles like “a” or “an.”

I bought a book. / She has three pens.
There are many ideas to consider.

Uncountable Nouns

These nouns refer to substances, concepts, or masses that cannot be counted directly. They do not have plural forms and are used with singular verbs.

We need more information.
Milk is good for you.
Tip: Use quantifiers like “some,” “much,” or “a lot of” with uncountable nouns. Use “many,” “a few,” or numbers with countable nouns.
