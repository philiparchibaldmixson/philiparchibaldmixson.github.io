Advanced Grammar – Mixed Singular and Plural

Understanding Mixed Singular and Plural

Some sentences contain both singular and plural nouns or pronouns. These structures require careful attention to agreement in verbs and pronouns. Errors often occur when the subject and complement differ in number or when collective nouns are involved.

Examples: Each of the students has a book. / The team are wearing their uniforms.

Common Challenges and Examples

Collective Nouns

The committee has made its decision. (treated as singular)
The committee are arguing among themselves. (treated as plural)

Indefinite Pronouns with Plural Reference

Everyone has their own opinion.
Each of the players is ready.

Mixed Subjects

Neither the teacher nor the students were late.
There is a book and two pens on the desk.
Tip: When subjects are mixed, the verb usually agrees with the nearest noun. Be consistent with pronoun reference.
