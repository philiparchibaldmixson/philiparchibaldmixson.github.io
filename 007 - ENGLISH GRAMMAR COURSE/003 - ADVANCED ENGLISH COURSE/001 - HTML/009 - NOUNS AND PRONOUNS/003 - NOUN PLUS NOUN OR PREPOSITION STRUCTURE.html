Advanced Grammar – Noun + Noun or Preposition Structure

Understanding Noun + Noun and Preposition Structures

In English, nouns are often combined to form compound expressions. These can appear as noun + noun (e.g., “chicken soup”) or as noun + preposition + noun (e.g., “a bowl of soup”). These structures help specify type, purpose, or relationship.

Noun + Noun: the first noun modifies the second
Noun + Preposition + Noun: shows possession, quantity, or category

Examples and Usage

Noun + Noun

I need a coffee mug. (a mug for coffee)
She bought a leather jacket. (a jacket made of leather)

Noun + Preposition + Noun

He drank a cup of tea.
They built a house on the hill.

Choosing Between Structures

A chicken soup vs. A bowl of soup
A student dorm vs. A dorm for students
Tip: Use noun + noun for compact, descriptive phrases. Use preposition structures for clarity or emphasis.
