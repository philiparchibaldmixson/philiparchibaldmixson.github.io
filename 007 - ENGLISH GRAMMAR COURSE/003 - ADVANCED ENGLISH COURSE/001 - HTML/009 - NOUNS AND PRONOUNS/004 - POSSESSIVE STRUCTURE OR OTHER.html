Advanced Grammar – Possessive Structure or Other Structure

Understanding Possessive and Alternative Structures

In English, possession or relationships between nouns can be expressed in two main ways: using the possessive form (with an apostrophe + s) or using a prepositional structure (usually “of”). Choosing between them depends on formality, clarity, and the type of noun.

Possessive: noun + ’s + noun (e.g., Maria’s book)
Prepositional: noun + of + noun (e.g., the book of Maria)

Usage and Examples

Possessive ’s

This is John’s car.
The teacher praised the student’s work.

“Of” Structure

The roof of the house was damaged.
The color of the sky changed quickly.

Choosing Between Them

Maria’s idea (more natural)
The idea of Maria (more formal or poetic)
Tip: Use ’s for people, animals, and time expressions. Use “of” for things, places, and abstract ideas.
