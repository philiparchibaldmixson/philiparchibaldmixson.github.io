Nouns and Pronouns

Advanced Grammar – One / Ones

Understanding “One” and “Ones”

One and ones are used as pronouns to avoid repeating a noun. They refer to something already mentioned or understood from context. “One” is singular, and “ones” is plural. These forms are common in comparisons, choices, and generalizations.

Examples: I like the red shirt, not the blue one. / These apples are fresher than the ones we bought yesterday.

Examples and Usage

Replacing Countable Nouns

Do you want the chocolate cake or the vanilla one?
I’ll take the small ones, please.

With Adjectives or Determiners

The expensive one is better quality.
I prefer the ones with less sugar.

In Generalization

One should always be prepared. (not related to noun replacement)
The ones who study hard usually succeed.
Tip: Use “one” or “ones” to avoid repetition and keep your sentences clear and concise.
