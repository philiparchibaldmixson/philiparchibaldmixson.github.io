Advanced Grammar – All

Understanding “All” as a Determiner

All is a powerful determiner used to refer to the entire quantity or group of something. It can be used with countable and uncountable nouns, and with pronouns. Word order and emphasis vary depending on the structure.

Examples: All students must attend. / All of the water was gone. / They all agreed.

Examples and Usage

With Countable Nouns

All books are on the shelf.
All of the employees arrived early.

With Uncountable Nouns

All sugar was used in the recipe.
All of the information is correct.

With Pronouns

We all enjoyed the concert.
They all passed the exam.
Tip: Use “all” before nouns or after pronouns. “All of” is used when followed by a determiner (e.g., the, my, these).
