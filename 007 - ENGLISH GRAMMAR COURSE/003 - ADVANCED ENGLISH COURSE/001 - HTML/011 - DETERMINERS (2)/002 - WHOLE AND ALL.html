Advanced Grammar – All and Whole

Understanding “All” and “Whole”

All and whole both express totality, but they differ in structure and emphasis. “All” is more flexible and used with plural and uncountable nouns. “Whole” is used with singular countable nouns and emphasizes completeness.

All: all students, all the water
Whole: the whole class, a whole cake

Examples and Usage

Using “All”

All children need care.
All of the money was spent.

Using “Whole”

She ate the whole apple.
We spent a whole day hiking.

Comparing “All” and “Whole”

All the cake was eaten. (focus on quantity)
The whole cake was eaten. (focus on completeness)
Tip: Use “all” with plural and uncountable nouns. Use “whole” with singular countable nouns and definite articles.
