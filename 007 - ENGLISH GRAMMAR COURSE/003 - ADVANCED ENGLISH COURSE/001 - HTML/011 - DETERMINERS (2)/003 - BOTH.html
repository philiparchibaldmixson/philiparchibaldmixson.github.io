Advanced Grammar – Both

Understanding “Both” as a Determiner

Both is used to refer to two people or things together. It emphasizes inclusion and is used with plural countable nouns, pronouns, and noun phrases. “Both of” is used when followed by a determiner (e.g., the, my, these).

Examples: Both students passed. / Both of my parents are teachers.

Examples and Usage

With Nouns

Both books are interesting.
Both of the options are valid.

With Pronouns

We both agreed on the plan.
They both arrived early.

Common Patterns

Both + noun (Both students)
Both of + determiner + noun (Both of the students)
Pronoun + both (We both, They both)
Tip: Use “both” to emphasize that two items or people are included. Use “both of” before determiners.
