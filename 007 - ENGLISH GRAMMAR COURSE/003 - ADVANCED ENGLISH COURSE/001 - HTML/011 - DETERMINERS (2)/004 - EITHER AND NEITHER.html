Advanced Grammar – Either and Neither

Understanding “Either” and “Neither”

Either and neither are determiners used to refer to two items. “Either” means one or the other, while “neither” means not one and not the other. They are used with singular countable nouns and can also appear as pronouns or conjunctions.

Either: Either option is fine. (one or the other)
Neither: Neither answer is correct. (not one, not the other)

Examples and Usage

With Singular Nouns

Either road leads to the station.
Neither solution worked.

With Pronouns

I don’t like either of them.
Neither of us knew the answer.

Common Patterns

Either + singular noun
Neither + singular noun
Either/Neither of + plural noun/pronoun
Tip: Use “either” for positive alternatives and “neither” for negative exclusion. Verb agreement depends on the noun.
