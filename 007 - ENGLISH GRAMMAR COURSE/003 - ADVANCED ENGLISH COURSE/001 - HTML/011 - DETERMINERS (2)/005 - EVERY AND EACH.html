Determiners

Advanced Grammar – Every and Each

Understanding “Every” and “Each”

Every and each are determiners used with singular countable nouns to refer to all members of a group. “Every” emphasizes the group as a whole, while “each” focuses on individual members separately.

Every: Every student must register. (group focus)
Each: Each student has a unique ID. (individual focus)

Examples and Usage

With Singular Countable Nouns

Every book in the library is catalogued.
Each book has a barcode.

With Pronouns and Quantifiers

Each of them received a certificate.
Every one of the participants was interviewed.

Common Patterns

Every + singular noun
Each + singular noun
Each of + plural noun/pronoun
Tip: Use “every” for generalizations and “each” for emphasis on individuality or detail.
