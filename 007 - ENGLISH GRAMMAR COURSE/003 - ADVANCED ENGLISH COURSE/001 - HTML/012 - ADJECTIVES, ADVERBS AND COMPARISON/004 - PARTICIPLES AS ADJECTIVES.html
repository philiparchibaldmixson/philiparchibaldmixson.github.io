Advanced Grammar – Participles Used as Adjectives

Understanding Participles as Adjectives

Participles are verb forms that can function as adjectives. The present participle ends in -ing and describes something that causes a feeling. The past participle usually ends in -ed and describes how someone feels.

Present participle: The movie was exciting.
Past participle: I was excited by the movie.

Examples and Usage

Present Participle (-ing)

The lecture was boring.
It’s a confusing puzzle.

Past Participle (-ed)

I felt bored during the lecture.
She was confused by the puzzle.
Tip: Use -ing for the thing that causes the feeling, and -ed for the person or thing that experiences it.
