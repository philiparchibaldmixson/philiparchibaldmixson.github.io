Advanced Grammar – -er/-est or More/Most?

Understanding -er/-est vs. More/Most

We use -er/-est for short adjectives (usually one syllable) and some two-syllable adjectives. We use more/most for longer adjectives (usually two or more syllables) and adverbs ending in -ly.

Examples:
She is taller than her brother. (short adjective)
This is the most beautiful He speaks more clearly

Examples and Usage

Using -er / -est

He is faster than me.
This is the coldest day of the year.

Using More / Most

She is more intelligent than her classmates.
That was the most exciting moment.
Tip: Use “-er/-est” for short words, “more/most” for longer ones. Some exceptions exist, so practice helps!
