Advanced Grammar – Double Comparative Structure

Understanding Double Comparatives

A double comparative is a structure that uses two comparative forms to show that one change causes another. It follows the pattern:

The + comparative, the + comparative
Example: The harder you work, the more successful you become.

This structure is used to express cause and effect or parallel increase/decrease between two ideas.

Examples and Usage

Common Patterns

The more you practice, the better you get.
The earlier we leave, the sooner we’ll arrive.
The less you worry, the happier you’ll be.
The more confident she feels, the more fluent she becomes.
Tip: Use “the” before each comparative. The second clause often shows the result of the first.
