Comparatives

Advanced Grammar – More About Comparatives

Understanding Comparatives in Depth

Comparatives are used to show differences between two things. While most follow the basic pattern (adjective + -er or more + adjective), there are exceptions, modifiers, and irregular forms to master.

Examples:
She is more careful than her brother.
This task is easier than the last one.
He is better at math than I am.

Examples and Usage

Regular Comparatives

This book is longer than the previous one.
She is more talented than her peers.

Irregular Comparatives

He is better at chess than his brother. (good → better)
This situation is worse than before. (bad → worse)

Modifiers with Comparatives

She is much taller than me.
This is slightly more complicated.
He’s far less confident today.
Tip: Use modifiers like much, a bit, far, slightly to adjust the degree of comparison.
