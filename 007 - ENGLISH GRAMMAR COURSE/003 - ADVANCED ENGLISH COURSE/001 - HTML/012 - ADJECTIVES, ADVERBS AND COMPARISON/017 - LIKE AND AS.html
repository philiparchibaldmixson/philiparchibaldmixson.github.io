Advanced Grammar – Like and As

Understanding “Like” and “As”

“Like” and “as” are both used to compare things, but they are used differently in structure and meaning.

LIKE is used to compare nouns or pronouns.
AS is used before a clause (subject + verb) or to describe a role or function.

Examples and Usage

Using “Like”

She sings like a professional singer. (comparison)
He looks like his father. (appearance)

Using “As”

She works as a teacher. (function/job)
Do it as I showed you. (clause)
Tip: Use “like” before a noun or pronoun. Use “as” before a clause or when describing a role.
