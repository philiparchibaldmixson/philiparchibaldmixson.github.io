Advanced Grammar – End-Position of Prepositions

Understanding End-Position of Prepositions

In formal English, prepositions usually come before their object. However, in spoken and informal English, prepositions often appear at the end of a clause or question—especially in relative clauses and questions.

Examples:
That’s the person I was talking to.
What are you looking at?
This is the topic we should focus on.

Examples and Usage

Questions Ending in Prepositions

Who are you talking to?
What is she afraid of?
Which team are they playing against?

Relative Clauses Ending in Prepositions

That’s the colleague I worked with.
This is the house we stayed in.
He’s the person I rely on.
Tip: In formal writing, place the preposition before the relative pronoun: “the person with whom I worked.” In informal speech, end-position is more natural.
