Advanced Grammar – Prepositions Before Conjunctions

Understanding Prepositions Before Conjunctions

Prepositions are typically followed by nouns or pronouns. However, in advanced structures, they can appear before conjunctions like that, whether, or if—especially in formal writing or speech. This usage often introduces clauses that function as the object of the preposition.

Examples:
We talked about whether we should continue.
He’s concerned about that she might leave.
She’s unsure of whether it’s the right decision.

Examples and Usage

Preposition + Conjunction + Clause

They argued about whether the plan was feasible.
I’m not sure of whether he understood the instructions.
She’s worried about that he won’t come.

Common Prepositions in This Structure

about, of, in, on, with, from, before, after
Tip: This structure is more common in formal contexts. In informal speech, we often rephrase to avoid placing a preposition before a conjunction.
