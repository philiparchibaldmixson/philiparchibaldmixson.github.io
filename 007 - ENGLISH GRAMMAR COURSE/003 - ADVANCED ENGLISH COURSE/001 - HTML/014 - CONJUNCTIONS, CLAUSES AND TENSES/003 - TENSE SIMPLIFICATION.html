Advanced Grammar – Tense Simplification After Conjunctions

What Is Tense Simplification?

In complex sentences with conjunctions like after, before, when, as soon as, and if, English often uses the present simple to refer to future time. This is called tense simplification.

Examples:
I’ll call you when I arrive. (not “when I will arrive”)
We’ll leave as soon as he finishes his work.

Common Conjunctions That Trigger Simplification

Time Conjunctions

After she graduates, she’ll travel abroad.
Before the movie starts, let’s get snacks.
As soon as I hear from him, I’ll let you know.

Conditional Conjunctions

If it rains, we’ll cancel the picnic.
Unless he calls, we won’t go.
Tip: Use the present simple after these conjunctions even when referring to the future.
