Relative Clauses

Advanced Grammar – Identifying and Non-Identifying Adjective Clauses

Understanding Relative Clauses

Relative clauses (also called adjective clauses) describe or give more information about a noun. They begin with relative pronouns like who, which, that, whose, or where.

Identifying Clauses: Essential to the meaning of the sentence. No commas.
Non-Identifying Clauses: Add extra information. Use commas.

Examples and Usage

Identifying Relative Clauses

The student who studies hard will succeed. (essential info)
Books that inspire me are on that shelf.

Non-Identifying Relative Clauses

My brother, who lives in Canada, is visiting next week. (extra info)
The Eiffel Tower, which is in Paris, attracts millions of tourists.
Tip: Use “that” only in identifying clauses. Use commas for non-identifying clauses.
