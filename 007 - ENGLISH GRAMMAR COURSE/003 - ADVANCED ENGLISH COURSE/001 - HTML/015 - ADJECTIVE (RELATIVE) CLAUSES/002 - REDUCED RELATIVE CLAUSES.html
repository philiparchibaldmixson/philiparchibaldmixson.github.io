Relative Clauses

Advanced Grammar – Reduced Relative Clauses

What Are Reduced Relative Clauses?

Reduced relative clauses are shorter forms of adjective clauses. They remove the relative pronoun and auxiliary verb, often using a participle instead. They are used to make writing more concise and formal.

Full Clause: The man who is talking to Mary is my uncle.
Reduced Clause: The man talking to Mary is my uncle.

Examples and Usage

Active Voice Reduction (Present Participle)

Students who study hard succeed. → Students studying hard succeed.
The woman who teaches math is my aunt. → The woman teaching math is my aunt.

Passive Voice Reduction (Past Participle)

The book that was written by Orwell is famous. → The book written by Orwell is famous.
The documents that were signed yesterday are missing. → The documents signed yesterday are missing.
Tip: Use present participles (-ing) for active clauses and past participles (-ed or irregular) for passive clauses.
