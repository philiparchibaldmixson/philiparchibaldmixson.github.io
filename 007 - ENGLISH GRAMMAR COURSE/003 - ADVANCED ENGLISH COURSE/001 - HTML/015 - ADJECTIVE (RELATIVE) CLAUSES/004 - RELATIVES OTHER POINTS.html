Advanced Grammar – Relatives: Other Points

Other Key Points About Relative Clauses

Relative clauses can be flexible and nuanced. Here are some advanced points to consider when using them:

1. Omission of Relative Pronouns: In identifying clauses, the relative pronoun can be omitted when it’s the object.
2. Use of “that”: “That” can replace “who” or “which” in identifying clauses, but not in non-identifying ones.
3. “Whose” for Possession: “Whose” can refer to people or things to show possession.
4. “Where” and “When”: These can be used to refer to place and time, respectively.

Examples and Usage

Omission of Relative Pronoun

The book (that) I read was amazing.
The person (who) she met is a writer.

Use of “Whose”

The student whose project won the prize is here.
The company whose logo changed is rebranding.

Use of “Where” and “When”

The café where we met is closed now.
The day when we arrived was rainy.
Tip: Use omission and substitution carefully to maintain clarity and grammatical accuracy.
