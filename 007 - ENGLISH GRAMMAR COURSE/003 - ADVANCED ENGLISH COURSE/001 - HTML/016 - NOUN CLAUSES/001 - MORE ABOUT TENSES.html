Understanding Tense Changes in Indirect Speech

In indirect speech, we often shift the tense back one step from the original direct speech. This is called backshifting. However, tense changes depend on the reporting verb and the context.

Direct: She said, “I am tired.”
Indirect: She said (that) she was tired.

Common Tense Shifts

Present → Past

“I like pizza.” → He said he liked pizza.

Present Perfect → Past Perfect

“I have finished.” → She said she had finished.

Past → Past Perfect

“I went home.” → He said he had gone home.

Will → Would

“I will call you.” → She said she would call me.
Tip: If the reporting verb is in the present (e.g., “says”), we usually keep the original tense.
