Advanced Grammar – More About That-Clauses

Understanding That-Clauses

A that-clause is a type of noun clause that begins with the word that. It often follows reporting verbs, adjectives, or nouns and functions as the object or complement in a sentence. In spoken English, “that” is often omitted, but in formal writing, it is usually included.

Structure: Main clause + that + subject + verb
Optional: “That” can be omitted when the clause is the object of the verb.

Examples and Usage

That-Clauses After Verbs

She believes that he is honest.
I think (that) we should leave now.

That-Clauses After Adjectives and Nouns

I’m glad that you came.
The fact that he apologized surprised everyone.

Omission of “That”

He said (that) he was tired.
We know (that) she’s right.
Tip: Avoid omitting “that” when it helps prevent ambiguity or improves clarity.
