Understanding Conditional Clauses with “If”

Conditional clauses express possible situations and their results. They begin with if and are followed by a main clause. There are four main types of conditionals, each with its own tense pattern and meaning.

Types: Zero, First, Second, Third Conditionals
Structure: If + condition clause, result clause

Types of Conditionals

Zero Conditional (General Truths)

If you heat water, it boils.
If people sleep well, they feel better.

First Conditional (Real Future Possibility)

If it rains, we will stay home.
If you study, you will pass.

Second Conditional (Unreal Present/Future)

If I were rich, I would travel the world.
If she studied more, she would succeed.

Third Conditional (Unreal Past)

If he had left earlier, he would have caught the train.
If we had known, we would have helped.
Tip: Pay attention to verb tenses. Each conditional has a distinct pattern and meaning.
