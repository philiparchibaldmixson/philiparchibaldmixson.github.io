Advanced Grammar – Unless

Understanding “Unless” Clauses

“Unless” introduces a condition that must be met to prevent the result from happening. It means “if not” and is used to express exceptions or negative conditions. It’s common in real and hypothetical situations.

Structure: Main clause + unless + condition
Meaning: “Unless” = “if not”

Examples and Usage

Real Conditions

You’ll fail unless you study.
We won’t go unless it stops raining.

Hypothetical or Formal Use

Unless he were here, we wouldn’t begin. (formal)
Unless you had told me, I wouldn’t have known. (past unreal)
Tip: Don’t use “unless” with negative verbs. Say “Unless you call” (not “Unless you don’t call”).
