Advanced Grammar – Notes on Some Conjunctions

Understanding Key Conjunctions in Adverb Clauses

Adverb clauses begin with subordinating conjunctions that show relationships like time, cause, contrast, condition, and purpose. Some conjunctions are similar in meaning but differ in usage, formality, or structure. Understanding these nuances helps you write with precision.

Common conjunctions: although, though, even though, while, whereas, because, since, as, so that, in order that, before, after, until, when, whenever, if, unless

Examples and Usage

Contrast Conjunctions

Although it was raining, we went out.
Even though he tried hard, he failed.
While I like coffee, I prefer tea.

Cause and Purpose Conjunctions

Because she was tired, she went to bed early.
He studied so that he could pass the exam.
I left early in order that I wouldn’t be late.

Time Conjunctions

After he arrived, we started dinner.
Until you finish, you can’t leave.
Whenever I call, she answers immediately.
Tip: Use “even though” for stronger contrast than “although.” Use “so that” for purpose, not result.
