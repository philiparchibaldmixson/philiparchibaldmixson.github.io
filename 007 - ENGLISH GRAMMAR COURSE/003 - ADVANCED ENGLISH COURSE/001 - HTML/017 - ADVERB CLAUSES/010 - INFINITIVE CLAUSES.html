Advanced Grammar – Infinitive Clauses

Understanding Infinitive Clauses

Infinitive clauses are reduced adverb clauses that use to + base verb to express purpose, result, or condition. These structures are concise and commonly used in formal writing. They often replace clauses beginning with “in order that,” “so that,” or “so.”

Structure: to + base verb (e.g., to succeed, to understand)
Function: Express purpose, intent, or result

Examples and Usage

Purpose and Intent

She studied hard to pass the exam. (in order that she could pass)
He left early to catch the train. (so that he could catch)

Result and Condition

The box was too heavy to lift. (so heavy that we couldn’t lift it)
He is smart enough to solve the problem. (so smart that he can solve it)
Tip: Infinitive clauses don’t have a subject. The subject must match the main clause.
