Advanced Grammar – Information Structure: What Comes First?

Understanding Information Structure

In advanced grammar, information structure refers to how we organize ideas in a sentence or across sentences. What comes first often depends on what is known (given) and what is new (important). English tends to place known information first and new information later.

Key principle: Start with what the listener already knows, then add new or important details.

Examples and Usage

Given → New

The meeting was canceled because the manager was sick.
As for the budget, it will be discussed tomorrow.

Topic → Comment

This book, I’ve read three times.
The weather today is perfect for a picnic.

Contrast and Emphasis

What I need is a break.
It was John who solved the problem.
Tip: Use cleft sentences and fronting to highlight contrast or focus.
