Advanced Grammar – Emphasis: What ... Is/Was Structure

What Is the “What ... Is/Was” Structure?

The “What ... is/was” structure is a type of pseudo-cleft sentence used to emphasize a specific part of a sentence. It allows writers to highlight actions, objects, or reasons by placing them after “What” and linking with “is” or “was.” This structure is common in formal writing and spoken emphasis.

Structure: What + clause + is/was + emphasized element
Purpose: Emphasis, clarification, contrast

Examples and Usage

Emphasizing Actions or Events

What she did was remarkable.
What surprised me was his reaction.

Emphasizing Reasons or Results

What caused the delay was the traffic.
What we need is more time.

Emphasizing Objects or Focus

What I want is a clear answer.
What they chose was unexpected.
Tip: Use “What ... is/was” to shift focus and make your writing more expressive and structured.
