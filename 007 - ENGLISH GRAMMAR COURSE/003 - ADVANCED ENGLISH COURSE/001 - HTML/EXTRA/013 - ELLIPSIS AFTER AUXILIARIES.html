Advanced Grammar – Ellipsis After Auxiliaries

What Is Ellipsis After Auxiliaries?

Ellipsis is the omission of words that are understood from context. In English, we often omit repeated verbs or verb phrases after auxiliary verbs (like do, be, have, will, can) to avoid repetition. This is common in comparisons, coordination, and responses.

Structure: Subject + auxiliary verb (ellipsis of repeated verb phrase)
Purpose: Avoid repetition, maintain fluency, and improve cohesion

Examples and Usage

In Coordination

She can play the piano, and he can too. (play the piano is omitted)
They have finished the project, but we haven’t. (finished the project is omitted)

In Short Answers

“Have you seen it?” – “Yes, I have.”
“Will they join us?” – “No, they won’t.”

In Comparisons

She works harder than he does. (works is omitted)
I’m more prepared than they are. (prepared is omitted)
Tip: Make sure the auxiliary verb matches the tense and subject of the omitted verb phrase.
