Advanced Grammar – Ellipsis with Infinitives

What Is Ellipsis with Infinitives?

Ellipsis with infinitives refers to omitting the repeated infinitive verb phrase when it’s understood from context. This is common in coordination, comparisons, and responses. It helps avoid redundancy and keeps writing concise and fluent.

Structure: Subject + infinitive marker (to) + [ellipsis of verb phrase]
Purpose: Avoid repetition, improve cohesion, maintain clarity

Examples and Usage

In Coordination

She wants to win, and I want to as well. (win is omitted)
They agreed to help, and we agreed to too. (help is omitted)

In Short Responses

“Do you plan to attend?” – “Yes, I hope to.”
“Will they try to negotiate?” – “They intend to.”

In Comparisons

He promised to work harder than she promised to. (work harder is omitted)
I expected to succeed, but didn’t manage to. (succeed is omitted)
Tip: Make sure the infinitive verb is clear from context and the ellipsis doesn’t cause ambiguity.
