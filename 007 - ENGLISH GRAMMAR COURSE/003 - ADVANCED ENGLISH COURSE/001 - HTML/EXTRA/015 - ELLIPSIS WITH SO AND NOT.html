Advanced Grammar – Ellipsis with So and Not

What Is Ellipsis with So and Not?

Ellipsis with “so” and “not” is a way to avoid repeating a verb phrase when agreeing or disagreeing with a previous statement. Instead of repeating the full verb phrase, we use “so” or “not” followed by an auxiliary verb and subject.

Structure: So/Not + auxiliary verb + subject
Purpose: Show agreement or contrast without repetition

Examples and Usage

Agreement with “So”

She is tired. So am I.
They have finished. So have we.

Disagreement with “Not”

He didn’t attend. Neither did I. / I did not either.
She won’t go. Nor will we.

Contrast with “So” and “Not”

He can speak French. So can she. But I can’t.
They should apply. So should you. But I shouldn’t.
Tip: Match the auxiliary verb and subject correctly. Use “neither” or “nor” for negative agreement.
