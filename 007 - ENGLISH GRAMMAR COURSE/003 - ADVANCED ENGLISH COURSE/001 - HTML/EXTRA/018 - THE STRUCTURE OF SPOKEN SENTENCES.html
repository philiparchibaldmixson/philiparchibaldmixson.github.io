Grammar Beyond the Sentence

Advanced Grammar – The Structure of Spoken Sentences

What Makes Spoken Sentences Different?

Spoken sentences often differ from written ones in structure, rhythm, and grammar. They tend to be shorter, less formal, and more interactive. Spoken grammar includes features like ellipsis, repetition, vague expressions, and discourse markers that help manage conversation and express emotion or attitude.

Key features: ellipsis, repetition, discourse markers, vague language, intonation, and turn-taking

Examples and Usage

Ellipsis and Repetition

“(Are you) Coming?”
“Yeah, yeah, I know.”

Discourse Markers

“Well, I’m not sure.”
“So, what do you think?”

Vague and Interactive Language

“Kind of tired, you know?”
“Like, it was weird.”
Tip: Spoken grammar reflects real-time communication. It’s flexible, context-dependent, and often incomplete—but still meaningful.
