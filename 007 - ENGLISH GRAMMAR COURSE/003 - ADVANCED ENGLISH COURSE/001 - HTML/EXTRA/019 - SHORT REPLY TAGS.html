Grammar Beyond the Sentence

Advanced Grammar – Short Answers, Reply Questions, and Question Tags

What Are These Structures?

Short answers, reply questions, and question tags are common in spoken English. They help maintain interaction, show agreement or surprise, and confirm information. These structures rely on auxiliary verbs and shared context to avoid repetition and keep conversation flowing.

Short answers: “Yes, I am.” / “No, she didn’t.”
Reply questions: “Did you?” / “Is she?”
Question tags: “You’re coming, aren’t you?” / “She didn’t call, did she?”

Examples and Usage

Short Answers

“Are you ready?” – “Yes, I am.”
“Did he leave?” – “No, he didn’t.”

Reply Questions

“I finished early.” – “Did you?”
“She’s moving abroad.” – “Is she?”

Question Tags

“You’ve met him, haven’t you?”
“She won’t mind, will she?”
Tip: Use the correct auxiliary verb and match the subject. Tags are positive after negative statements and vice versa.
