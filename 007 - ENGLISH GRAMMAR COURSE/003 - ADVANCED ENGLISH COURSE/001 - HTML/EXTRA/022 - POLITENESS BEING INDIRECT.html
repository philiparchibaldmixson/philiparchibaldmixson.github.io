Advanced Grammar – Politeness: Being Indirect

What Does It Mean to Be Indirect?

Indirectness is a key strategy in polite communication. Instead of stating something directly, speakers use hints, suggestions, or softened language to avoid sounding rude, demanding, or confrontational. This is especially important in requests, refusals, criticism, and disagreement.

Common strategies: hedging, vague language, modal verbs, indirect questions, conditionals
Purpose: Maintain harmony, show respect, reduce pressure

Examples and Usage

Indirect Requests

“I was wondering if you could help me.”
“Would it be possible to reschedule?”

Indirect Refusals

“I’m not sure I’ll be able to.”
“That might be a bit difficult right now.”

Indirect Criticism

“Maybe we could try a different approach.”
“I’m not sure that’s the best idea.”
Tip: Use modal verbs like “might,” “could,” and “would,” and soften tone with phrases like “I was wondering” or “It seems.”
