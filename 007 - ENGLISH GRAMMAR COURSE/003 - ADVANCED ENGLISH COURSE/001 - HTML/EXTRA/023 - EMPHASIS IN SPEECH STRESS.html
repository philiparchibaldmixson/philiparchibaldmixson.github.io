Advanced Grammar – Emphasis in Speech: Stress

What Is Stress in Speech?

Stress in spoken English refers to emphasizing certain words or syllables to highlight meaning, emotion, or contrast. It helps listeners understand what’s important in a sentence. Stress can change the meaning depending on which word is emphasized.

Types of stress: contrastive stress, emotional stress, focus stress
Purpose: Highlight key information, express attitude, clarify meaning

Examples and Usage

Contrastive Stress

“I said SHE could go.” (not someone else)
“He DID call.” (contradicting doubt)

Emotional Stress

“I REALLY need your help.”
“This is SO frustrating!”

Focus Stress

“What she wants is a BREAK.”
“The problem is the TIMING.”
Tip: In writing, stress is shown with italics, bold, or capitalization. In speech, it’s heard through pitch, volume, and length.
