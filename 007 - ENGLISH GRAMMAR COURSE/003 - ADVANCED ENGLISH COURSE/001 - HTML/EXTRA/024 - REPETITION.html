Advanced Grammar – Repetition in Discourse

What Is Repetition in Discourse?

Repetition is a cohesive device used to reinforce meaning, maintain topic continuity, and emphasize key ideas. In spoken and written discourse, repetition can involve repeating words, phrases, or structures to guide the listener or reader through the message.

Types: lexical repetition, structural repetition, paraphrased repetition
Purpose: Emphasis, cohesion, clarification, rhetorical effect

Examples and Usage

Lexical Repetition

“This is important. Really important.”
“We need results. Results that matter.”

Structural Repetition

“She came, she saw, she conquered.”
“What you see is what you get.”

Paraphrased Repetition

“He was exhausted. In other words, he couldn’t go on.”
“The idea was simple. That is, easy to understand.”
Tip: Repetition can be powerful, but overuse may sound redundant. Use it purposefully to guide emphasis and cohesion.
