Grammar Beyond the Sentence

Advanced Grammar – New Headlines

What Are New Headlines?

New headlines refer to the abbreviated, compressed style used in news titles. They often omit auxiliary verbs, articles, and subjects to save space and create impact. This style is common in newspapers, online media, and broadcast journalism.

Features: present simple for past events, omission of “be,” use of noun phrases, verbless clauses
Purpose: Brevity, immediacy, attention-grabbing

Examples and Usage

Compressed Grammar

“Government approves new tax plan” (instead of “The government has approved a new tax plan.”)
“Storm hits coast” (instead of “A storm has hit the coast.”)

Omission of Articles and Verbs

“Man wins lottery” (instead of “A man has won the lottery.”)
“Prices rise again” (instead of “The prices are rising again.”)

Noun Phrases as Headlines

“New vaccine rollout”
“Economic recovery plan”
Tip: Headlines use present tense for recent events and omit non-essential words. Context fills in the grammar.
